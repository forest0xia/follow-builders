**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck** — OpenAI's Compute Chief: We Can't Build Fast Enough | Sachin Katti

**The Takeaway:** Every time OpenAI thought it had enough compute and eased off, it was wrong — and the physical world simply doesn't build fast enough to keep up with AI's appetite.

Sachin Katti is OpenAI's Head of Industrial Compute — the person overseeing what many call the largest infrastructure build in human history. His background spans Stanford (professor of CS and EE), several startups, and Intel (where he was CTO). At OpenAI, his team's job is the entire lifecycle: land, power, shells, chips, financing, construction, operations, and capacity allocation.

The most counterintuitive insight from Katti: the training/inference distinction is increasingly meaningless. Synthetic data generation, post-training, and test-time compute are all inference — even within what gets called a "training run." This means inference is likely already the majority of OpenAI's compute consumption.

On the physical build: OpenAI's data centers are "giant factories turning electrons into tokens" — football-field-scale liquid-cooled supercomputers where everything, including the cables and transformers, needs active cooling. The company doesn't just connect to the grid; it funds new generation and transmission infrastructure in every region it builds, arguing this is a net positive for communities, modernizing grid infrastructure that would otherwise never be funded.

Katti is candid that bottlenecks are everywhere: permitting, gas turbines, transformers, and critically, skilled labor — electricians and plumbers. Nuclear, he says, "can't come soon enough."

On Jalapeno, OpenAI's custom ASIC (built with Broadcom): it went from concept to tape-out in nine months, partly because OpenAI knows the exact workload it's optimizing for, and partly because AI itself is now helping accelerate chip design iteration cycles. The key metric: tokens per watt.

On networking: OpenAI's MRC protocol sprays packets across all available paths in a 100,000-GPU cluster, masking failures so training runs aren't interrupted — the network becomes an abstracted, always-available resource.

The bigger picture: tokens of intelligence are becoming a supply unit. OpenAI's "guaranteed capacity" product commits tokens to enterprise customers the way utilities commit power. Katti sees no world where compute demand eases — if anything, AI doing AI research exponentially increases the number of experiments that can be run, which demands more compute still.

"The physical world does not move that fast. We do believe that the world of recursion is not that far where AI will design the systems it needs to train and run the next generation of AI."
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wEZBlmvxx4o

**关键结论：** OpenAI 每次以为算力够用而放慢脚步时，事后都证明是错的——而物理世界的建设速度根本跟不上 AI 的胃口。

Sachin Katti 是 OpenAI 工业计算主管——许多人认为他正在监管人类历史上最大规模的基础设施建设。他的背景横跨斯坦福大学（计算机科学与电气工程教授）、多家初创公司，以及英特尔（担任 CTO）。在 OpenAI，他的团队负责整个生命周期：土地、电力、机房壳体、芯片、融资、施工、运营以及算力分配。

Katti 最出人意料的洞察是：训练与推理的区别正在变得越来越无意义。合成数据生成、后训练、测试时计算——都是推理，即便在所谓的"训练阶段"也是如此。这意味着推理很可能已经占据了 OpenAI 算力消耗的大多数。

在物理建设层面：OpenAI 的数据中心是"将电子转化为 token 的巨型工厂"——足球场规模的液冷超级计算机，连电缆和变压器都需要主动冷却。公司不只是接入电网，而是在每个建设区域自主投资新的发电和输电基础设施，认为这对当地社区是净利好，现代化了本来不会得到资金的电网。

Katti 坦承瓶颈无处不在：审批许可、燃气轮机、变压器，以及最关键的技术工人——电工和管道工。他说，核能"越快来越好"。

关于 Jalapeno，OpenAI 与 Broadcom 合作的定制 ASIC：从概念到流片仅用了九个月，部分原因在于 OpenAI 清楚地知道要优化的工作负载，也部分因为 AI 本身正在帮助加速芯片设计的迭代周期。核心指标：每瓦 token 数。

在网络层面：OpenAI 的 MRC 协议将数据包分散到 10 万 GPU 集群中所有可用路径上，优雅地掩盖故障，使训练任务不受中断——网络成为一种抽象的、始终可用的资源。

更大的图景：智能 token 正在成为一种供应单元。OpenAI 的"保证容量"产品像电力公司承诺供电一样，向企业客户承诺 token 配额。Katti 认为没有任何情况下算力需求会放缓——事实上，AI 进行 AI 研究会以指数级增加可运行的实验数量，这反而需要更多算力。

"物理世界的运转没那么快。但我们确实相信，'递归'时代已经不远了——AI 将设计它用于训练和运行下一代 AI 所需的系统。"
