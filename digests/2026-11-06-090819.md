**Unsupervised Learning: Ep 97: Jev Creator on Decision Models, Benchmark Fatigue & Why the Labs Missed It**

**The Takeaway:** The JEV CEO argues that most AI labs are optimizing for benchmarks and demos rather than real-world reliability — and that this benchmark theater has already exhausted developer trust faster than anyone expected.

The JEV model — built by Jogo, CEO of TypeSave — took the AI world by storm roughly two months ago with a radically different approach: instead of generating strings for humans to read, JEV outputs structured data for machines to consume. The philosophy: LLMs are fundamentally "system one" thinkers — fast and instinctive — and the right move is to be honest about that rather than dress them up as general-purpose reasoners.

What makes Jogo's perspective unusual is his frustration not with AI's limits, but with how the field responds to them. He believes developers are stuck in a kind of Stockholm syndrome — passively accepting whatever model providers ship — rather than pushing back on reliability gaps that should be fixed. His most memorable line about Anthropic's upcoming IPO: "The 'let them cure cancer' is going to be like Marie Antoinette's 'let them eat cake.'" He sees valuations as partly driven by misunderstandings of what AI can actually do, not just product optimism.

On coding agents, he estimates a 20–40% productivity boost on average — "a decent but small boost" — with significant caveats around skill degradation and tech debt. He's genuinely excited about two unsolved problems: coding agents that break free from the KV cache (which forces all state management to be append-only and limits what multi-agent systems can do), and semantic dependent typing — a future where programs carry natural-language constraints that AI models can verify.

His most provocative claim: models from the GPT-3.5 era "could have met the definition of AGI" (economically valuable work by volume) — the raw intelligence was already there, we just never learned to use it.

**Unsupervised Learning 第 97 期：Jev 创始人谈决策模型、Benchmark 疲劳，以及为什么各大实验室错过了这一切**

**核心观点：** JEV CEO 认为，大多数 AI 实验室在为 benchmark 和演示优化，而非真实世界的可靠性——这种"benchmark 表演"已经比任何人预期的都更快地耗尽了开发者的信任。

JEV 模型由 TypeSave CEO Jogo 构建，约两个月前以截然不同的理念震撼了 AI 界：它不输出供人类阅读的字符串，而是输出供机器消费的结构化数据。背后的哲学是：LLM 本质上是"系统一"思维者——快速、本能——正确的做法是对此坦诚，而不是将其包装成通用推理引擎。

Jogo 视角的独特之处，不在于对 AI 局限性的抱怨，而在于他对这个领域应对局限方式的失望。他认为开发者陷入了一种斯德哥尔摩综合症——被动接受模型提供商的输出——而不是对本应修复的可靠性缺口发出反馈。他关于 Anthropic 即将 IPO 最令人难忘的一句话："'让他们治愈癌症'就像玛丽·安托瓦内特的'让他们吃蛋糕'。"他认为这些估值部分建立在对 AI 真实能力的误解之上，而不仅仅是产品层面的乐观。

在编程 agent 方面，他估计平均生产力提升在 20-40%——"一个不错但幅度有限的提升"——并对技能退化和技术债务问题有所保留。他真正兴奋的两个尚未解决的问题是：摆脱 KV cache 束缚的编程 agent（KV cache 强制所有状态管理采用追加写入模式，限制了多 agent 系统的能力），以及语义依赖类型系统——一个未来程序可以携带自然语言约束、由 AI 模型来验证的世界。

他最具挑战性的观点：GPT-3.5 时代的模型"本可以达到 AGI 的定义"（按经济价值量衡量的工作自动化）——原始智能早已具备，我们只是从未学会如何使用它。
