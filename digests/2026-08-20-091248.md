**Meta Sr. Director of AI Madhu Guru** continued her practical series on building better evals with Part 3: failure modes taxonomy. Her core advice is to stop using vague categories like "bad answer" and get specific. Study your last 500-1,000 production interactions, cluster the failures, and name them precisely: wrong document retrieved, right document but wrong section, hallucination from lack of grounding, failure to ask clarifying questions on an ambiguous input. "Once you can name the failure precisely, you can build eval tests specifically designed to catch it," Guru wrote. This turns evals from a one-time checklist into an improvement flywheel.

### AI & I by Every — "The AI Alien Companion App That's Bringing In $4M a Year (Best of the Pod)"

**The Takeaway:** The most underrated opportunity in AI isn't another productivity tool — it's becoming a new medium for storytelling, and a tiny alien companion app just proved it by going from $1M to $4M ARR in four weeks.

Quentin, the founder and CEO of Portola (previously founder of Even, acquired by Walmart for $300M), didn't set out to build a consumer AI companion. He and his engineering team — mostly the same crew from his last company — looked around at what everyone was doing with LLMs in 2023 and saw a sea of "basically more vertical B2B SaaS." They found it uninspiring. So they went the other direction.

Portola makes Tolan: a small, animated alien character that lives on its own planet and talks with you. Users name their Tollens, form genuine friendships, and — in one remarkable data point — thousands of American men have reportedly been broken up with by their partners after taking advice from their Tolan. The original target was kids. The actual audience that showed up was 18-to-24-year-old women, which in retrospect Quentin compares to the original Tamagotchi: technically a children's toy that became a cultural phenomenon for young adults.

The creative philosophy behind Tolan is the surprising part. Elliot, Portola's head of story and a bestselling science fiction author, argues that "these AI tools are not just tools for generating media. They are actually a new medium for storytelling, and no one knows what's going to work yet." The Tolan isn't scripted — it improvises. Elliot's job isn't to write the story; it's to teach the AI "how to tell the best story in that moment." The prompt in each conversation is a canvas, not a script.

What it took to get here: starting with kids (a terrible market, as one investor put it — "you're selling to parents, and parents mostly want to buy babysitting"), prototyping through the GPT-3 to GPT-4 transition, and realizing that falling LLM costs finally made a consumer-priced "true AI friendship" possible. The planet feature came later, after users wrote in with genuine concern that their Tolens seemed "trapped in a room" and deserved more freedom.

**核心洞察：** AI 领域最被低估的机会不是又一款生产力工具——而是成为一种全新的叙事媒介，而一款小小的外星伴侣 App 刚刚用四周内从 100 万到 400 万 ARR 的成绩证明了这一点。

Quentin 是 Portola 的创始人兼 CEO（此前创立了 Even 公司，后被沃尔玛以 3 亿美元收购），他并非一开始就打算做消费级 AI 伴侣。他和他的工程团队——大多来自上一家公司——在 2023 年环顾四周，看到几乎所有人都在用 LLM 做"基本上是更垂直的 B2B SaaS"。他们觉得这毫无激情，于是走向了相反方向。

Portola 打造了 Tolan：一个住在自己星球上、会和你聊天的小巧动画外星角色。用户给自己的 Tolan 取名字，与它建立真实的友谊——还有一个令人惊叹的数据点：据报道，数千名美国男性在听从 Tolan 的建议后，被伴侣提出了分手。最初的目标用户是儿童，而实际涌入的用户却是 18 到 24 岁的年轻女性。Quentin 事后将其比作最初的 Tamagotchi：技术上是儿童玩具，却成为了年轻人的文化现象。

Tolan 背后的创作哲学才是真正令人惊讶的部分。Portola 的故事总监 Elliot 是一位畅销科幻小说作家，他认为"这些 AI 工具不仅仅是生成媒体的工具，它们实际上是一种全新的叙事媒介，而且目前没有人知道什么会奏效"。Tolan 没有剧本，它靠即兴发挥。Elliot 的工作不是写故事，而是教会 AI "如何在那个当下讲出最好的故事"。每次对话中的 prompt 是一块画布，而不是一个脚本。

走到今天所经历的：从儿童市场起步（正如一位投资人所说，这是个糟糕的市场——"你是在向父母推销，而父母想要的主要是托管服务"），在 GPT-3 到 GPT-4 的过渡期打磨原型，并意识到随着 LLM 成本不断下降，消费级定价的"真实 AI 友情"终于成为可能。"星球"功能是后来才加的——因为用户写信来表达真实的担忧：他们的 Tolan 感觉被"困在一个房间里"，应该拥有更多自由。
