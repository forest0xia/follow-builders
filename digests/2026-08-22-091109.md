### Madhu Guru — Sr Director of AI at Meta (formerly led Gemini, Veo, Nano Banana at Google)

In part 5 of his "How to build great evals" series, Madhu Guru takes aim at a habit he calls "the tyranny of the average" — collapsing a rich eval suite into a single number to satisfy executives. He shows exactly why this is dangerous: a model can improve from 85% to 89% on simple summarization while dropping from 70% to 63% on complex financial analysis. A single score buries the regression. His fix: keep a prioritized list of evals, find people who can sit with the details rather than abstracted simplicity, and make holistic judgment calls rather than weighted-average math tricks.

在"如何构建优秀 eval"系列的第 5 篇中，Madhu Guru 炮轰了一种他称为"均值暴政"的陋习 — 为了满足高层对简洁数字的需求，把丰富的 eval 套件压缩为单一分数。他用具体案例说明这有多危险：一个模型在简单摘要任务上从 85% 提升到 89%，同时在复杂金融分析上从 70% 跌至 63%，一个总分彻底掩盖了这次退步。他的建议：保持按优先级排列的 eval 清单，找能深入细节而非追求抽象简洁的人，做出整体判断，而非依赖加权平均的数字把戏。

### No Priors — What Chess.com Teaches Us About Superhuman Capabilities, with CEO Erik Allebest

**The Takeaway:** Chess — a game where computers have been superhuman for 30 years — is more popular than ever, offering a living proof that humans will keep caring deeply about human skill even after machines surpass them.

Erik Allebest bought the chess.com domain out of bankruptcy in 2005 for $56,000 when every investor told him it was an uninvestable niche. Two decades later, Chess.com has 250 million members, $200M in annual revenue, and 10 million daily active users — a business built entirely without outside capital until private equity came calling. The real growth unlock came not from fundraising but from cultural moments: COVID + The Queen's Gambit in 2020, then a second wave in 2023 driven by short-form content and the Hans Niemann cheating scandal.

**On why machines haven't killed human chess:** "Humans want to do human stuff." When early chess engines like Stockfish made perfect play boring and grinding, neural net engines like Leela Chess Zero came along playing aggressively and unconventionally — making chess more exciting than ever. AI coaches now help players review their games, generate personalized puzzles, and learn faster. Allebest's conclusion: machines made chess better, not irrelevant.

**On expertise and the one insight that doesn't change:** "Take any given day and multiply it by a thousand — that's what your life looks like." Whether it's 5 chess puzzles per day or 100, the compounding of small daily habits is what separates experts. Access to AI accelerates the learning loop but doesn't replace the repetitions.

**On cheating:** Chess.com has been fighting engine-assisted cheating longer than most industries have dealt with AI fraud. Their edge is data — they know exactly what human play looks like vs. computer play, and their statistical models get sharper over time. "Cheating can't be prevented because you can't put a webcam in every person's home. But we do a really good job at catching it."

Chess.com is now expanding into other games under gambit.com (poker), bringing the same rating-system philosophy: how good are you really, beyond chip counts and bluffing power?

**关于超人能力，Chess.com 教给我们的事 — No Priors 播客，嘉宾：Chess.com CEO Erik Allebest**

**核心洞见：** 国际象棋 — 一项计算机已称霸 30 年的游戏 — 如今比以往更受欢迎。这是一个活生生的证明：即便机器超越了人类，人们依然会深深在意人类自己的技艺。

Erik Allebest 在 2005 年以 5.6 万美元从破产拍卖中买下 chess.com 域名，彼时每个投资人都说这是个"不值得投"的小众爱好。二十年后，Chess.com 拥有 2.5 亿注册用户、每年 2 亿美元营收、1000 万日活 — 全程自力更生，没有外部融资，直到私募股权登门。真正的增长爆发来自文化事件：2020 年的 COVID 和《后翼弃兵》，以及 2023 年由短视频内容和 Hans Niemann 作弊丑闻引发的第二波浪潮。

**关于机器为何没有扼杀人类棋局：** "人类想做人类的事。" 早期国际象棋引擎 Stockfish 让完美棋局变得无聊沉闷，神经网络引擎 Leela Chess Zero 随后横空出世 — 风格激进、出其不意 — 让国际象棋比以往任何时候都更精彩。AI 教练现在帮助棋手复盘对局、生成个性化练习题、加速学习过程。Allebest 的结论：AI 让国际象棋变得更好，而非过时。

**关于专业技能的那条永恒规律：** "把你某一天乘以一千，那就是你的人生轮廓。" 无论是每天做 5 道棋谜还是 100 道，小习惯的复利才是专家与普通人的分界线。AI 可以加速学习循环，但无法代替那些必须亲自完成的重复训练。

**关于作弊：** Chess.com 与引擎辅助作弊的战斗，比大多数行业面对 AI 欺诈早了很多年。他们的优势在于数据 — 他们清楚地知道人类下棋和计算机下棋的样本差异，统计模型随时间持续精进。"作弊无法完全预防，因为你不可能在每个人家里装摄像头。但我们非常擅长发现它。"

Chess.com 现正通过 gambit.com 扩展至其他棋牌游戏（目前是扑克），沿用同样的评级哲学：你真正的实力如何，而非你有多少筹码或多会虚张声势？
