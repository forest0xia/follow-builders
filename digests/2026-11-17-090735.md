### Latent Space: [One Million AI Engineer Special] How Stanford Teaches AI-Native Engineering — Mihail Eric, Monaco

Latent Space celebrated one million subscribers on the AI Engineer YouTube channel with a special episode featuring Mihail Eric — Adjunct Professor at Stanford teaching CS 146S "The Modern Software Developer" and Head of AI at Monaco. The episode is a sweeping tour of what it means to teach and practice AI-native engineering in 2026.

Eric's course — now in its second year — was born from a 2024 realization that the methodology of AI-assisted development was scattered across blog posts with no structured curriculum. The course covers the full stack of modern AI engineering: prompt engineering, context windows, coding agents, background agents, evals, security, and the emerging practice of multi-agent systems at scale.

A standout segment examined the evolution of Claude Code's system prompt — once famously 20,000 tokens long — which has since been cut dramatically, with capabilities like Git tooling baked into the model weights rather than spelled out in the prompt. Eric also demoed Monaco's internal AI toolchain: Monocoder (a background agent platform built on Coder), and Hal, an internal Slack bot that serves as a combined AISRE, QA engineer, and customer success agent — with different "personalities" depending on whether it's talking to a salesperson or a developer.

The episode closed with career advice for the next cohort of AI engineers: build benchmarks and evals. It gets you in the door of every frontier lab. And if you turn them into products, there's real money there too — SWE-bench, after all, was an undergrad project from Princeton.
