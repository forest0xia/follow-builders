**Aaron Levie (Box CEO)**

Box CEO Aaron Levie laid out a nuanced framework for thinking about automation risk: the "hardest" fields — math, cybersecurity, code — are actually most vulnerable to AI automation first, precisely because they're verifiable. Clear reward signals in training, and testable outputs in deployment. Conversely, domains like legal, marketing, sales, and finance have no "one right answer" — they depend on changing context, operator judgment, and outcomes that can't be measured for months. His conclusion: even as models improve exponentially, there will be enormous applied AI work to do, and the processes themselves will need to evolve.
https://x.com/levie/status/2083965372747882741

Box CEO Aaron Levie 提出了一个关于自动化风险的细致分析框架：看似"最难"的领域——数学、网络安全、编程——实际上最先面临 AI 自动化的冲击，原因恰恰在于它们可验证。训练时有清晰的奖励信号，部署时输出结果可以客观测试。反过来，法律、市场、销售、财务等领域没有"唯一正确答案"——它们依赖于不断变化的语境、决策者的判断，以及有时几个月后才能评估的结果。他的结论：即便模型能力呈指数级提升，应用层的 AI 工作量依然巨大，业务流程本身也需要随之演进。

**Dan Shipper (Every CEO)**

Every CEO Dan Shipper mapped out a theory of "agency ruptures" that's worth saving. When AI can suddenly do something that used to require you, it triggers an identity crisis — "you are no longer required, and you had no say in the matter. It's a kind of death." But the rupture follows a predictable three-stage arc: first you only see the AI (and forget the human scaffolding); then you start to see the scaffolding; finally, you reconstruct agency around your new role and the AI fades into invisible infrastructure. His leading indicator for success in the AI economy: the ability to metabolize these ruptures as curiosity rather than existential threat. He also noted that technology doesn't just change what we can do — it reshapes what we ought to do, meaning AI will fundamentally alter moral intuitions over time.

**Training Data: "Building the Automated AGI Lab: Core Automation's Jerry Tworek and Rohan Anil"**

**The Takeaway:** The founders of Core Automation — both from the inner circles of OpenAI and Google DeepMind respectively — believe the transformer architecture has fundamentally hit its ceiling for the most important capability: learning from deployment. Building the replacement is why they started a company.

Jerry Tworek led the Strawberry and reasoning teams at OpenAI as VP; Rohan Anil was one of four pre-training leads on Gemini and the inventor behind the Shampoo optimization algorithm. Between them, they've seen more of frontier AI from the inside than almost anyone alive. Their joint diagnosis is direct: transformers are economically valuable and scientifically impressive, but they cannot learn after deployment. The training-lab-to-real-world gap is fundamental, not a tuning problem.

Tworek's argument is architectural. Scaling RL worked — benchmarks went up — but real-world task coverage didn't follow. The reason: training data never fully replicates the open-ended messiness of deployment. The fix isn't more RL rollouts or better prompting. It's a new architecture that can learn at test time, on a user's actual distribution, continuously. "We need models that learn with users on their data, on their real world task, on the real world distribution." Neither in-context learning (limited to ~20 minutes of context before compaction) nor continuous fine-tuning (catastrophic forgetting) cuts it. Something architecturally new is needed.

Anil brings the optimization perspective: today's transformers are shallow (at most ~100 layers deep), spend computation poorly, and the band-aid of chain-of-thought inference-time scaling is inefficient — one token at a time just to increase computational depth. His view: optimization and architecture are two sides of the same coin, and whoever finds an architecture that spends compute like biology does — learning continuously without catastrophic forgetting — will define the next era.

Core Automation's six-month roadmap focuses on kernel generation: automating the translation from novel architectural ideas into hardware-optimized GPU kernels. Their QR kernel competition demonstrated the scale of the gap — a human expert spending $100K and four weeks with current coding agents achieved a kernel 60x faster than off-the-shelf. Current frontier models can't come close to that. Closing this loop automatically is their inner loop for finding a better architecture faster.

On AGI: Tworek defines it as a model that can improve itself without humans in the loop — not just economically valuable, but genuinely self-improving. By that definition, nothing today is close. "The human-LLM hybrid is really, really successful right now, but LLMs without humans — not so much. Not at all."

**要点：** Core Automation 的联合创始人——分别来自 OpenAI 和 Google DeepMind 核心圈子——相信 transformer 架构已经从根本上触及了最关键能力的天花板：从部署中持续学习。为了找到替代方案，他们选择出来创业。

Jerry Tworek 曾以 VP 身份领导 OpenAI 的 Strawberry 和推理团队；Rohan Anil 是 Gemini 四位预训练负责人之一，同时是 Shampoo 优化算法的发明者。两人合计见过的前沿 AI 内部运作，比几乎任何在世的人都要多。他们的联合诊断直截了当：transformer 在经济价值和科学成就上无可挑剔，但它们无法在部署后继续学习。训练实验室与真实世界之间的鸿沟是根本性的，不是调参能解决的问题。

Tworek 的论点落在架构层面。RL 规模化起效了——各项基准分数上升了——但真实世界的任务覆盖并没有跟上。原因在于：训练数据永远无法完整复现部署环境中开放性的混乱。解决方案不是更多 RL rollout 或更好的 prompt 工程，而是一种全新架构，能够在测试时、在用户真实的数据分布上持续学习。"我们需要能与用户共同学习的模型——在他们的数据上、在他们的真实任务上、在他们的真实分布上。"无论是上下文学习（压缩前约 20 分钟的上下文限制）还是持续微调（灾难性遗忘），都无法解决这个问题。需要的是架构层面的全新突破。

Anil 从优化视角切入：今天的 transformer 很浅（最多约 100 层），算力利用效率低，而思维链推理时缩放不过是个临时方案——逐 token 生成只是为了增加计算深度，非常低效。他的判断：优化方法和架构是同一枚硬币的两面，谁能找到一种像生物大脑那样利用算力的架构——持续学习且不发生灾难性遗忘——谁就将定义下一个时代。

Core Automation 的六个月路线图聚焦于 kernel 自动生成：将新架构创意自动转化为硬件优化的 GPU kernel。他们举办的 QR kernel 竞赛直观展示了这一差距的规模——顶尖人类专家花费 10 万美元和四周时间，借助当前编程 agent，最终实现了比现成方案快 60 倍的 kernel。现有前沿模型完全无法做到。把这个循环自动化，是他们更快找到更优架构的核心飞轮。

关于 AGI：Tworek 将其定义为一个无需人类介入即可自我改进的模型——不只是具有经济价值，而是真正能够自我进化。以这个标准衡量，当今什么都离得很远。"人类-LLM 混合模式现在非常非常成功，但没有人类的 LLM——远远不够。完全不够。"
