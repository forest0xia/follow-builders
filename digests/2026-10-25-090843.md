**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck**
*Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text — and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)*

**The Takeaway:** Diogo Almeida, CEO of TypeSafe and co-inventor of RLHF, argues that the entire generative AI field has been optimizing for the wrong thing — and that a new class of "machine-native" models trained to make decisions rather than generate text will unlock the automation that LLMs promised but never delivered.

Almeida's thesis starts with a blunt diagnosis: RLHF, the training method he helped invent at OpenAI, made models fluent and pleasing but unreliable. "95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean. And if it doesn't tell you when it's the Mr. Bean, then how are you gonna automate anything?" RLHF optimizes for human approval, which means outputs always look confident and coherent — even when they're wrong. That polished surface created an enormous overpromise-underdeliver gap for automation use cases.

His company's answer is JEV (named after Jevons Paradox, the economic principle that cheaper resources get consumed at higher volumes), which outputs probabilities and structured decisions instead of prose. The insight is that classifiers — long dismissed as old-school ML — are actually the interface through which intelligence enters software. Every major platform (Google, TikTok, Facebook) runs on classifiers. What TypeSafe has done is train a model specifically to be a *general-purpose* classifier that runs on top of the rich compressed knowledge in pretrained LLMs: all the understanding, none of the text-generation overhead.

The business case is composability. Reliable decision primitives can be stacked: a classifier you trust can be called a hundred times inside a higher-order workflow. "Code is kind of like the roller coaster tracks. You don't go off the rails." The copycats who emerged within three weeks of TypeSafe's launch, he says, are likely building the same thing he built in week one — something that looks right but fails at reliability. The hard work is in the training recipe, the data, and the iterative calibration, none of which is visible in the interface.

On RSI and AI winters: Almeida is skeptical of recursive self-improvement narratives ("people don't understand diminishing returns in the real world") but not bearish on AI. His worry is more specific — that AI will keep being "incredibly underwhelming" at automation while being celebrated for chat. "Pre-training is a giant dumpster fire of wasting resources. So I hope to never do so."

The "Jevolution," as he calls it, isn't about replacing LLMs. It's about opening a second market — intelligence inside software, not intelligence beside it.

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck**
*Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text — and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)*

**核心洞见：** TypeSafe CEO、RLHF 共同发明人 Diogo Almeida 认为，整个生成式 AI 领域一直在优化错误的目标——一类全新的"机器原生"模型，被训练为做出决策而非生成文本，将解锁 LLM 承诺却从未兑现的自动化潜力。

Almeida 的论点从一个直白的诊断开始：他在 OpenAI 参与发明的 RLHF 训练方法，让模型变得流利而讨人喜欢，却牺牲了可靠性。"95% 爱因斯坦，5% 憨豆先生。如果它不告诉你什么时候是憨豆先生，你怎么可能去自动化任何事情？"RLHF 优化的是人类的认可，这意味着输出总是看起来自信而连贯——哪怕是错的。这种精致的表面，造成了自动化场景中巨大的过度承诺-实际落差。

他的公司的答案是 JEV（得名于 Jevons 悖论——资源越便宜，消耗量越大），它输出概率和结构化决策，而非散文。核心洞见是：分类器——长期被视为旧式机器学习——实际上是智能进入软件的接口。每个主要平台（Google、TikTok、Facebook）都运行在分类器之上。TypeSafe 所做的，是训练一个专门用于通用分类的模型，它建立在预训练 LLM 丰富的压缩知识之上：保留了所有理解能力，去掉了文本生成的开销。

商业逻辑在于可组合性。可信赖的决策原语可以层层叠加：一个你信任的分类器可以在更高阶的工作流中被调用一百次。"代码就像过山车的轨道，不会脱轨。"他说，在 TypeSafe 发布三周内涌现的模仿者，很可能在构建他第一周就构建过的东西——看起来正确，但在可靠性上失败。真正的难点在于训练方案、数据和迭代校准，这些在界面上根本看不见。

关于 RSI 和 AI 寒冬：Almeida 对递归自我改进叙事持怀疑态度（"人们不理解现实世界中的收益递减"），但并不看空 AI。他的担忧更具体——AI 在聊天上受到追捧，却在自动化上持续"令人失望"。"预训练是一场浪费资源的垃圾火灾，我希望永远不必这么做。"

他所说的"Jev 革命"，不是替代 LLM，而是开辟第二个市场——让智能存在于软件内部，而非软件旁边。
