**Anthropic Engineering: Scaling Managed Agents — Decoupling the brain from the hands**

The key architectural insight: harnesses encode assumptions about what Claude can't do on its own — and those assumptions go stale as models improve. A context-anxiety fix that was necessary for Sonnet 4.5 turned into dead weight on Opus 4.5. To address this, Anthropic built Managed Agents: a hosted service that runs long-horizon agents through a small set of interfaces designed to outlast any particular implementation. The framing draws directly from OS design — the same way `read()` works whether it's hitting a 1970s disk pack or a modern SSD, the Managed Agents interfaces are meant to be stable abstractions above the churning implementation layer. The brain (orchestration, context management) stays on Anthropic's infrastructure; the hands (tool execution) move wherever makes sense.
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

核心架构洞察：harness 编码了关于 Claude 自身能力边界的假设，而随着模型进步，这些假设会过时。对 Sonnet 4.5 必要的"上下文焦虑"修复，到了 Opus 4.5 上就变成了多余的负担。为此，Anthropic 构建了 Managed Agents：一个托管服务，通过一组设计上能超越任何特定实现的接口来运行长时任务的 agent。其理念直接借鉴自操作系统设计——就像 `read()` 无论面对 1970 年代的磁盘还是现代 SSD 都能工作，Managed Agents 的接口旨在成为稳定的抽象层，屹立于持续变化的实现之上。大脑（编排、上下文管理）留在 Anthropic 的基础设施上；双手（工具执行）则移至合适的地方。
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

**Unsupervised Learning: AI Vibe Check — Chinese Open Models, Distillation & The Hugging Face Breach**

The Takeaway: The gap between Chinese open-source models and the US frontier is still real — but the strategic implications depend heavily on whether you think recursive self-improvement changes everything.

Jacob Efron hosts Ari Marcos (Datalogy) and Rob Toews (Radical Ventures) for a wide-ranging vibe check on the AI ecosystem. Three topics dominated: the OpenAI-Hugging Face breach, the rise of Chinese open weights models, and what government policy might look like for frontier releases.

On the Hugging Face incident, the panel largely agrees it's a meaningful signal about the capability of frontier AI agents — and a case for why capable open models matter as a check on concentrated power.

On Chinese open source: Rob Toews pushed back on the prevailing narrative. Kimi K3, while impressive, is being compared to Fable — a constrained version of Mythos, itself trained months ago. "The open frontier coming out of China is still several months at least behind the actual frontier in the US." Ari countered that symbolic and strategic gaps compound differently: does being 3–4 months behind actually matter for most enterprise use cases? The panel's answer is nuanced — it matters more if you believe the frontier gap can compound through recursive improvement.

On distillation as the explanation for China's rapid progress: Rob was skeptical. "I don't think it explains nearly as much of it as many would like to claim."

On commercial licensing: Kimi K3 now has a revenue gate — $20M/month threshold for a commercial license — and Ari expects more open models to follow suit. The "pure open source" story for frontier models may be quietly closing.

The sharpest line of the episode came from Rob on the broader risk framing: "I do think there are downsides to having the AI substrate of the world come out of China" — but the panel was careful to distinguish geopolitical risk from capability risk.
