**No Priors — Superintelligence Needs the Physical World with CEO and Co-Founder of Arda Bob McGrew**

**The Takeaway:** Automating researchers won't trigger recursive self-improvement -- because researchers were never the bottleneck in the first place.

Bob McGrew, CEO and co-founder of Arda and former Chief Research Officer at OpenAI, makes a counterintuitive argument that stops a lot of AI acceleration narratives cold: the path to superintelligence runs through the physical world, not just the digital one.

His core point is that GPUs -- not researchers -- have always been the real bottleneck in AI research. Even during his tenure as CRO at OpenAI in 2024, the limiting factor wasn't headcount but the physical compute available. So automating researchers is a one-time acceleration (like the jump from GPT-4o to o3), not a recursive self-improvement loop. As he put it: "The difference between going from 90 to 99% of optimal is actually not a big difference. We can automate researchers -- it's a big one-time benefit, but it doesn't kick in a self-improvement cycle."

What does unlock the next phase? Making the physical world keep pace with cheap intelligence. When hypothesis generation becomes cheap, the scarce resource shifts to wet labs, factories, and GPU arrays needed to run the actual experiments. This is why McGrew founded Arda: to build the AI platform for manufacturing engineering -- starting with a partnership with defense manufacturer Androl to let engineers design and reconfigure automated assembly lines faster. Early results: a manufacturing engineer who normally took days to build a virtual model of an actuator did it in 90 minutes using their software.

The vision scales further. McGrew sees the next ten years as a "tremendous perfusion" of AI-driven perception and manipulation into physical form factors -- not just humanoid robots, but drones, four-legged machines, and specialized tools, all directed by AI. "As we have all these AGIs, right now humans are the eyes and hands of the intelligence that's in the data center," he says. The goal is to liberate that intelligence so it can go build things, terraform for humanity, and eventually explore the stars. That route runs through manufacturing.

**No Priors — Superintelligence Needs the Physical World with CEO and Co-Founder of Arda Bob McGrew**

**核心观点：** 自动化研究员不会触发递归自我改进——因为研究员从来都不是瓶颈所在。

Bob McGrew，现任 Arda CEO 兼联合创始人、前 OpenAI 首席研究官，提出了一个让很多 AI 加速叙事戛然而止的反直觉论断：通往超级智能的道路必须穿越物理世界，而不仅仅是数字世界。

他的核心观点是：GPU——而非研究员——才一直是 AI 研究的真正瓶颈。即使在他 2024 年担任 OpenAI CRO 期间，制约因素也不是招募的人才数量，而是可用的物理计算资源。因此，自动化研究员只是一次性加速（就像从 GPT-4o 跳跃到 o3 那样），而非递归自我改进循环。正如他所说："从最优的 90% 提升到 99%，差距其实并不大。我们可以自动化研究员——这是一次重大的一次性收益，但它不会触发自我改进的循环。"

那么什么才能解锁下一阶段？让物理世界跟上廉价智能的步伐。当假设的生成变得廉价，稀缺资源就转移到了湿实验室、工厂和用于运行实验的 GPU 阵列上。这正是 McGrew 创立 Arda 的原因：为制造工程打造 AI 平台——从与国防制造商 Androl 的合作开始，让工程师能更快地设计和重新配置自动化装配线。早期成果：一位制造工程师用他们的软件在 90 分钟内完成了通常需要数天的致动器虚拟建模。

这个愿景还在继续延伸：McGrew 预见未来十年将出现 AI 驱动的感知与操控能力向各种物理形态的"大规模渗透"——不只是人形机器人，还有无人机、四足机器、各类专用工具，全部由 AI 调度指挥。"随着越来越多的 AGI 出现，现在是人类充当数据中心里智能的眼睛和双手，"他说。目标是解放这种智能，让它去建造事物，为人类改造世界，最终探索星际。而这条路，从制造业开始。
