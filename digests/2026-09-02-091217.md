**Training Data: "Making Cities Awesome: Peregrine's Nick Noone & Ben Rudolph"**

**The Takeaway:** The most important layer for making cities safer isn't surveillance — it's data governance — and Peregrine is betting that decentralized, locally-owned intelligence is both the ethical and the commercially viable path.

Nick Noone and Ben Rudolph, co-founders of Peregrine, came at public safety from opposite directions: Noone did forward-deployed engineering inside Palantir's SOCOM unit, embedded in high-stakes intelligence operations in the Middle East; Rudolph worked with UNHCR on refugee crises in Africa and India before joining a last-mile healthcare startup. They converged on the same thesis: the people doing the most important institutional work — police departments, fire services, emergency management — were drowning in data they couldn't use, managed inside systems that didn't talk to each other.

Peregrine's core product insight is what Noone calls the "anti-network-effect" model. Where incumbents like Flock and Axon built their businesses by accumulating customer data, Peregrine deliberately does the opposite: "We are actually not in any shape or form in the business of bringing more data to the customer that they don't already have. The fundamental problem is that they can't utilize the data that they have." Every agency owns its own data. Peregrine provides permissioning, governance, and integration.

The practical results are striking. A Florida county was baffled by over 100 water rescues in a single month. Peregrine's agentic analysis traced the pattern to three consecutive days of unusual weather creating sand channels — conditions that produce rip currents — across data sets including 911 calls, incident reports, and weather feeds. A detective investigating threats against synagogues needed semantic search, not keyword search, to surface related incidents — something Peregrine's AI made possible across tens of thousands of records.

Their most ambitious project is a "cold case agent" that processes 200-300 GB of mixed-media evidence files (video, audio, images, PDFs) over 30-60 minute autonomous runs. They validated it by reproducing the same exoneration result detectives had previously reached manually. Now deployed in several U.S. departments, it recently helped place a suspect at a crime scene from scattered cell records buried in 300 GB of Wisconsin case files.

Noone's leadership philosophy runs against Silicon Valley's instinct to take credit: "Being the quiet professionals in a context like this and empowering the customer is why we have access to the next problem." The forward-deployed team is explicitly treated as R&D and growth, not a cost center — product primitives get built because engineers in the field build workarounds that make the real need obvious.

**Training Data：「如何让城市变得更美好：Peregrine 的 Nick Noone 和 Ben Rudolph」**

**核心观点：** 让城市更安全的最重要一层不是监控，而是数据治理——Peregrine 正在押注，去中心化、本地所有权的智能，既是合乎伦理的路径，也是商业上可行的路径。

Nick Noone 和 Ben Rudolph，Peregrine 的联合创始人，从截然不同的方向走向公共安全：Noone 在 Palantir 的 SOCOM 部门做前线部署工程，深入中东高风险情报行动；Rudolph 在 UNHCR 参与非洲和印度的难民危机应对，后加入一家最后一公里医疗初创公司。两人最终汇聚于同一个判断：承担最重要机构工作的人——警察部门、消防队、应急管理机构——正淹没在无法使用的数据中，这些数据分散在各个互不相通的系统里。

Peregrine 的核心产品洞察是 Noone 所称的"反网络效应"模式。Flock 和 Axon 等老牌玩家通过积累客户数据构建业务，Peregrine 刻意反其道而行之："我们根本不从事为客户带来他们原本没有的数据的业务。根本问题在于，他们无法有效利用自己已有的数据。"每个机构拥有自己的数据，Peregrine 提供权限管理、数据治理和集成能力。

实际成果令人印象深刻。佛罗里达某县在一个月内发生了超过 100 次水上救援，原因不明。Peregrine 的 agentic 分析追溯到一个规律：连续三天异常天气形成沙洲，产生了适合激流的条件——这一发现横跨 911 呼叫记录、事故报告和天气数据多个数据集。一名调查针对犹太教堂威胁的侦探需要语义搜索而非关键词搜索，才能从数万条记录中找到相关事件——Peregrine 的 AI 让这成为可能。

他们最具雄心的项目是一个"冷案 agent"，能在 30-60 分钟的自主运行中处理 200-300 GB 的混合媒体证据文件（视频、音频、图片、PDF）。他们通过重现侦探此前手动完成的无罪释放结论来验证这套系统。目前已在美国多个警察部门部署，最近协助从埋藏在威斯康星州 300 GB 案卷中的零散手机记录里，锁定了嫌疑人在犯罪现场的位置。

Noone 的领导哲学与硅谷抢占功劳的本能背道而驰："在这种场合做低调的专业人士、赋能客户，正是我们能够获得下一个问题的原因。"前线部署团队被明确定位为研发和增长，而非成本中心——产品基础能力得以构建，恰恰是因为一线工程师在现场建了变通方案，让真正的需求变得显而易见。
