**AI & I by Every (now The Every Podcast) — "How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time"**

**The Takeaway:** Sam Altman believes AI's greatest promise isn't automation that replaces people — it's a renaissance that gives people control of their time, creativity, and lives back.

Speaking at OpenAI's Dev Day, Altman reflected on the surprising resilience of human-centered work despite rapid AI advances. Demand for developers has only grown with better coding models — not shrunk — because people want more things, not fewer. "People's expectations are so high. And I think that's a wonderful thing."

Altman drew a sharp contrast between the Industrial Revolution and a potential AI Renaissance. The Industrial Revolution raised living standards but turned many people into specialized cogs in machines, disconnecting them from the things that made life meaningful. His hope for AI: "People get to... back in control of their lives and ability to kind of do the things they want and spend their time the way they want."

His most personal example was Dots, a new product OpenAI launched at Dev Day. Altman used to love his early mornings for deep work — but running a large company meant checking his phone the moment he woke up to catch overnight problems, which polluted the rest of his morning. Dots gave him that back: "I trust my dot to go have good judgment about what I need to think about... and I get this space for creativity or to just hang out with my kids back. It sounds small, but like a significant improvement to my life."

In a striking live example, Dots flagged a technical issue five minutes before a keynote demo that could break the presentation — and offered to try to fix it. It couldn't yet, but Altman noted it was close.

On building a platform: Altman admitted OpenAI hasn't figured out its App Store equivalent, but has figured out how to help millions of people build significant new products. His framing: the world is no longer bottlenecked by what can be built, but by the "creative spirit to figure out what to build" — which is why he's pushing hard on ecosystem openness.

**要点：** Sam Altman 认为，AI 最大的承诺不是取代人类的自动化，而是一场文艺复兴，让人们重新掌控自己的时间、创造力和生活。

在 OpenAI Dev Day 上，Altman 反思了人类中心工作在 AI 快速发展下表现出的韧性。更强的编程模型并没有减少对开发者的需求，反而增加了——因为人们想要的东西变多了，而不是变少了。"人们的期待非常高。我认为这是一件美好的事情。"

Altman 将工业革命与潜在的 AI 文艺复兴做了鲜明对比。工业革命提高了生活水平，却让许多人变成了机器上的专业零件，与生活中真正有意义的部分脱节。他对 AI 的期望是："人们能够重新掌控自己的生活，能够做自己想做的事，按照自己想要的方式支配时间。"

他给出了最私人的例子：OpenAI 在 Dev Day 发布的新产品 Dots。Altman 过去喜爱早晨时光，那是他做深度工作的最佳时段，但掌管一家大公司意味着他每天一醒来就得看手机查看夜间问题，这污染了他整个上午的状态。Dots 把这一切还给了他："我信任我的 dot 来判断什么需要我关注……我重新拥有了创造力的空间，或者只是陪孩子待一会儿。听起来很小，但对我的生活是一个显著的改善。"

在一个生动的现场案例中，Dots 在主题演讲演示前五分钟标记了一个可能导致崩溃的技术问题，并主动提出要修复它。这次还没能做到，但 Altman 认为已经很接近了。

关于平台建设，Altman 坦承 OpenAI 还没找到自己的 App Store，但已经找到了帮助数百万人构建重要新产品的方式。他的框架是：这个世界不再受制于"能构建什么"，而是受制于"找出要构建什么的创造精神"——这也是他大力推进生态开放的原因。
