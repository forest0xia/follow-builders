## PODCASTS

**Latent Space: Controlling Biology — Fabian Theis, Helmholtz Munich**
https://www.youtube.com/@LatentSpacePod

**The Takeaway:** AI is now advanced enough to close the loop in biological research — from mapping cells, to predicting their behavior, to actively steering what the next experiment should be.

Fabian Theis, who leads AI and computational biology at Helmholtz Munich and created widely-used tools like Scanpy and scVerse, joined the Latent Space AI for Science podcast to discuss the state of virtual cell research and what's coming next.

The key distinction Theis draws: most computational biology has moved through two phases — *mapping* (building atlases of cell types) and then *predicting* (what will a cell do given a certain input?). The field is now entering a third phase: *steering*. In this phase, AI tells you what experiments to run in order to reach a desired cell state. His lab's tool for this, called Lab Compass, is an inverse generative model that works backward from a target biological outcome to prescribe the interventions needed to get there. The long game, he says, is full reinforcement learning in the lab loop — using AI not just to analyze data, but to actively decide and execute the next experiment.

What's driving this transition? Data scale. Theis described a key inflection: perturbation data — the space of things you can do to a cell (knock out a gene, add a drug, change media conditions) — used to be sparse. Now, single experiments can capture 10 million transcriptomes, and companies are running thousands of perturbations across hundreds of cell lines. That richness finally makes perturbation prediction models viable and powerful.

He's especially excited about organoids — tiny multicellular systems (mini-brains, beating heart clusters) grown in a dish from reprogrammed cells. Unlike single-cell lines, organoids model how cells communicate with each other, making them far closer to real tissue. Theis sees organoid perturbation data as the critical frontier for training the next generation of virtual cell models.

**Latent Space：控制生物学——Fabian Theis，慕尼黑亥姆霍兹研究中心**
https://www.youtube.com/@LatentSpacePod

**核心洞见：** AI 已足够成熟，可以闭合生物学研究循环——从细胞图谱绘制，到行为预测，再到主动引导下一个实验方向。

Fabian Theis 是慕尼黑亥姆霍兹研究中心 AI 与计算生物学负责人，也是 Scanpy、scVerse 等被广泛使用工具的创建者。他在 Latent Space AI for Science 播客中深入探讨了虚拟细胞研究的现状与未来。

Theis 提出了一个清晰的阶段框架：计算生物学已历经*绘图*（构建细胞类型图谱）和*预测*（细胞在特定输入下会如何响应）两个阶段，现在正进入第三阶段——*引导*。在这一阶段，AI 不仅能预测结果，还能反向告诉研究者"要达到某种细胞状态，你需要做哪些实验"。他的实验室开发了名为 Lab Compass 的逆向生成模型，正是这一思路的落地产品。他的终极愿景是在实验室循环中引入完整的强化学习——让 AI 不只分析生物数据，而是主动决策并执行下一步实验。

推动这一转变的是数据规模的爆发。Theis 描述了一个关键拐点：扰动数据（即你可以对细胞施加的各种操作——基因敲除、加药、改变培养条件等）以前非常稀缺；现在单次实验可捕获 1000 万个转录组，企业正在数百个细胞系上进行数千种扰动的高通量筛查。这种数据密度终于让扰动预测模型成为可能。

他对类器官（organoids）尤为兴奋——这是在培养皿中由重编程细胞生长出的微型多细胞系统（如迷你大脑、跳动的心脏细胞团）。与单一细胞系不同，类器官能模拟细胞间的通讯，更接近真实组织。Theis 认为，类器官扰动实验将成为训练下一代虚拟细胞模型不可或缺的前沿数据来源。
