**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text - and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)**

The Takeaway: Diogo Almeida, CEO of TypeSafe AI, argues that large language models were never the right tool for automation — and that a fundamentally different kind of AI, one that outputs decisions instead of text, is the actual key to making AI useful inside software.

Almeida is the creator of Jev, arguably the fastest-growing AI model of 2026. After two years in stealth and a launch video that hit 40 million views, TypeSafe closed an $870M Series A at a $7.5B valuation. But the story Almeida tells isn't really about growth — it's about a gap he identified while still at OpenAI, where he co-invented RLHF.

His diagnosis: RLHF, the training method that made ChatGPT feel so impressive, inadvertently optimized for surface-level human pleasing rather than actual decision reliability. Models became charming, confident, and wrong in ways that weren't obviously wrong — making them spectacular at impressing users and terrible at running autonomously in the background. "It's 95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean. And if it doesn't tell you when it's the Mr. Bean, how are you gonna automate anything?"

Jev's approach — which Almeida calls "machine-native AI" — strips out autoregressive text generation and instead returns calibrated probabilities. Developers get structured outputs (e.g., "is this email urgent? 0.87") that can be composed like logic gates. This makes AI radically cheaper, faster, and most importantly composable: the key property that has made software so powerful for decades. "You can now express things that you couldn't express in software before."

He's skeptical of the "copycats" who emerged within weeks of Jev's launch — Cloudflare, Databricks, Microsoft, and OpenAI — arguing that copying the interface is trivial but that years of work went into the actual reliability of the underlying models. He's equally dismissive of benchmarks: "Benchmarks are lies." He wants users to measure what actually matters — whether the system runs reliably in production.

On the bigger picture: Almeida doesn't believe in recursive self-improvement, and isn't worried about the AI hype cycle crashing the field. His concern is simpler — that AI will continue to be useless at real automation. His bet: Jevons Paradox will play out in intelligence the way it did for semiconductors. As Jev gets cheaper and more composable, people will build more with it, not less.

**播客：MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — Jev：做决策而非生成文字的 AI，以及它为何改变一切（Diogo Almeida）**

核心观点：TypeSafe AI CEO Diogo Almeida 认为，大型语言模型从来都不是自动化的合适工具，一种输出决策而非文字的全新 AI，才是让 AI 真正在软件中发挥价值的关键。

Almeida 是 Jev 的创始人，可以说是 2026 年增长最快的 AI 模型。在两年隐秘研发、发布视频突破 4000 万次观看之后，TypeSafe 以 75 亿美元估值完成了 8.7 亿美元的 A 轮融资。但 Almeida 讲述的故事并非关于增长，而是他在 OpenAI 期间识别到的一个巨大缺口 -- 他正是在那里共同发明了 RLHF。

他的诊断：RLHF，这个让 ChatGPT 令人印象深刻的训练方法，无意中优化了表面讨人喜欢的特质，而非实际的决策可靠性。模型变得迷人、自信，但以不那么明显的方式出错 -- 打动用户效果出众，但后台自主运行一塌糊涂。"它 95% 是爱因斯坦，5% 是憨豆先生。如果它不告诉你什么时候是憨豆先生，你怎么能把任何事情自动化？"

Jev 的方法 -- Almeida 称之为"机器原生 AI" -- 去掉了自回归文本生成，转而返回校准过的概率值。开发者获得结构化输出（例如"这封邮件紧急吗？0.87"），可以像逻辑门一样组合使用。这使得 AI 在价格和速度上大幅提升，最重要的是具备了可组合性：这正是几十年来软件如此强大的关键属性。"你现在可以表达以前无法在软件中表达的东西。"

他对 Jev 发布后几周内涌现的"仿制者"持怀疑态度 -- Cloudflare、Databricks、Microsoft 和 OpenAI -- 认为复制界面很容易，但模型实际可靠性背后有多年的工程积累。他对基准测试同样不屑一顾："基准测试是谎言。"他希望用户衡量真正重要的东西 -- 系统是否在生产环境中可靠运行。

关于更宏观的图景：Almeida 不相信递归自我改进，也不担心 AI 炒作泡沫会摧毁这个领域。他的担忧更为简单 -- AI 将继续在真正的自动化上毫无用武之地。他的赌注：Jevons 悖论将在智能领域重演，就像它在半导体领域所做的那样。随着 Jev 越来越便宜、可组合性越来越强，人们将用它构建更多，而非更少。
