**AI & I by Every: "Best of the Pod: Wired's Kevin Kelly on Why AI Is a 50-year Overnight Success"**

**The Takeaway:** Intelligence is a compound, not an element — and we're as ignorant of its nature today as Isaac Newton was about electricity in his time.

Kevin Kelly, founding editor of Wired and longtime technology futurist, offers a reframe that cuts through AI hype: our confusion about what intelligence actually is mirrors how early scientists were baffled by electricity. Newton — one of history's greatest minds — was "totally wrong" about electricity, clinging to pre-scientific notions. Kelly argues the same is true of intelligence: "I suspect that intelligence is not an element, but a compound. In the same way that salt is actually a compound of some elements that they had not yet identified, you can think of the current AI as we're making some kind of salt, and we don't even know what it's made from."

The implication: rather than converging on one type of AI, we will produce hundreds of distinct forms of intelligence, filling out a vast "possibility space of all possible minds." We are an edge species — our intelligence is a peculiar mixture evolved for our circumstances, not the universal center of what intelligence can be.

On why VR still hasn't taken off: "You could say VR is still waiting for its LLM moment" — its breakthrough will likely come from an unexpected direction, just as reasoning emerged unexpectedly from language translation. On robotics: the biological constraints on working near human-scale bodies make the timeline far longer than people expect.

Kelly also explores AI as a creative collaborator: he co-built 10 interconnected novels with AI featuring Leonardo da Vinci, Martin Luther, and Columbus — not to share them, but purely for the joy of creation. "The joy of creating it was better than reading it." He sees AI-generated content as largely "audience of one" — 99.999% of AI images are made for the co-creator's pleasure alone. This is not a business model; it's a new form of self-expression, like Sunday painting. On mastering AI: "This is a skill that will take your ten thousand hours."

**AI & I by Every：《最佳播客：〈连线〉杂志 Kevin Kelly 谈 AI——五十年磨一剑的"一夜成名"》**

**核心洞见：** 智能是化合物，不是元素——我们今天对智能本质的认知，不亚于牛顿时代对电的无知。

《连线》杂志创始编辑、长期技术未来学家 Kevin Kelly 提供了一个穿透 AI 噱头的重新框架：我们对"智能究竟是什么"的困惑，正如早期科学家对电感到茫然。牛顿——历史上最伟大的头脑之一——对电"完全搞错了"，执着于前科学时代的错误观念。Kelly 认为智能亦然："我怀疑智能不是元素，而是化合物。就像盐实际上是由一些尚未被识别的元素构成的化合物，你可以把当今的 AI 理解为我们正在制造某种盐，而我们甚至不知道它由什么构成。"

其含义是：我们不会收敛于一种 AI，而将产生数百种截然不同的智能形态，填满巨大的"所有可能思维的可能性空间"。人类是边缘物种——我们的智能是为我们的特定处境而进化的独特混合物，并非智能的普世中心。

关于 VR 为何至今仍未起飞："可以说 VR 还在等待它的 LLM 时刻"——突破很可能来自意想不到的方向，就像推理能力意外地从语言翻译中涌现一样。关于机器人：在接近人体尺度的环境中工作所面临的生物学约束，使其实现时间线远比人们预期的要长。

Kelly 还探讨了 AI 作为创作伙伴的角色：他与 AI 共创了 10 部相互关联的小说，主角包括达芬奇、马丁·路德和哥伦布——不为分享，只为创作的乐趣本身。"创作的喜悦胜过阅读它。"他将 AI 生成的内容定位为大多是"受众为一"——99.999% 的 AI 图像是为共同创作者自身的乐趣而生成的。这不是商业模式，而是一种新型自我表达，如同周日绘画。关于掌握 AI："这是一种技能，要从中获益最大，需要你投入整整一万小时。"
