**AI & I by Every — "How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time"**

**The Takeaway:** Sam Altman believes AI is sparking a new Renaissance — one centered on human agency and creativity — not an Industrial Revolution where people become cogs in a machine, and his personal use of OpenAI's new "Dots" feature is his clearest example of what that future feels like from the inside.

Every's podcast (recently rebranded from AI & I) kicked off its new chapter with Sam Altman at OpenAI's Dev Day. The conversation weaves together how Altman thinks about AI's place in history, what OpenAI is building for developers, and what it's actually like to use the tools they're shipping.

On the big picture, Altman draws a sharp distinction: the Renaissance was centered on people; the Industrial Revolution turned them into specialized cogs. He's determined that AI go the Renaissance route. "I get sad to hear people talk about AI automating everything... We're going to be like house cats and the AI will decide how to reward us. That's certainly not what we think." Instead, he sees AI as expanding human potential — letting people reconnect to what makes life good while also exploring science, art, and company formation.

The most personal insight comes from Dots, OpenAI's new proactive agent. Altman describes how it restored his favorite part of the day: "The Dots is the first thing that has given me back control of my mornings and my mental space in the mornings." Previously, checking his phone for urgent issues contaminated his deep-work window. Now he trusts his Dot — named Dottie — to exercise judgment about what actually needs him, handle the rest, and surface only the critical things at the right moment. One concrete example: five minutes before a Dev Day keynote demo, his colleague's Dot spotted an issue in the demo setup and proactively offered to fix it.

On developers, Altman is more bullish than ever despite — or because of — rising model capabilities: "People just want so much more stuff. People's expectations are so high." More developers than ever, more work than ever. On platforms, he's candid that OpenAI hasn't cracked the App Store equivalent yet, but argues they've enabled millions of people to build significant products.

He also addresses speed as the new frontier. OpenAI launched an "Ultrafast" mode and is investing in its own chips: "There will always be a real frontier in terms of intelligence or speed... but you can also bet that we will keep doing what we've done of just a ridiculous, relentless focus on driving value per dollar up."
