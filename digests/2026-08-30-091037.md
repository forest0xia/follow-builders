### No Priors — "From Restoring Sight to Reimagining the Brain, with Max Hodak"

**The Takeaway:** The brain is a computer — and once you truly believe that, a quiet revolution in medicine becomes possible.

Max Hodak, founder and CEO of Science and a co-founder of Neuralink, doesn't speak in polite abstractions. "The brain very literally, very clearly, plainly is a computer," he says. "You can solve computational problems by arranging matter in a certain way and then taking your hands off and pressing go." Starting from that premise, Science built Prima — a subretinal implant that restores a form of vision to patients who have gone completely blind. In July 2026, Prima received marketing approval in Europe, making it commercially available. The first sales will happen in the coming weeks.

**뇌는 컴퓨터다——그 전제 하나가 의학의 조용한 혁명을 가능하게 한다.**

Science 창업자이자 CEO인 Max Hodak（曾是 Neuralink 联合创始人）不讲客套话。他说："大脑在字面意义上、非常清晰地就是一台计算机。你可以通过特定方式排列物质，然后放手让它运行，从而解决计算问题。" 从这个前提出发，Science 开发了 Prima——一种植入视网膜下的芯片，能让完全失明的患者恢复视觉。2026 年 7 月，Prima 在欧洲获得上市许可，开始商业化销售，首批销售将在未来几周内完成。

Prima works like a cochlear implant for the eye: a tiny chip implanted under the retina receives a projected image from smart glasses, bypassing dead photoreceptors to stimulate the retina directly. Hodak is blunt about where the product stands: "It's like looking through a straw. It's only black and white." But patients in the clinical trial were filling in sudoku puzzles and reading books. Nobody had ever restored a coherent visual image to a blind patient's mind's eye before. The engineering path to better — adding grayscale depth, then some color — is clear.

Prima 的工作原理类似眼睛版的人工耳蜗：一枚微型芯片植入视网膜下，通过智能眼镜投射图像，绕过已死亡的感光细胞直接刺激视网膜。Hodak 对产品现状直言不讳："现在的视野像从吸管里看出去，而且只有黑白。" 但临床试验中，已有患者能做数独和读书。此前从未有人能在失明患者的脑海中恢复清晰的视觉图像。改进路径也清晰可见：先增加灰度深度，再引入色彩。

What makes Hodak's worldview distinctive is the Platonic representation hypothesis — the idea that AI models and biological brains converge on the same underlying representations of concepts. "When you look inside these big AI models, the mathematical objects you see look a lot like the things you see in neuroscience." Science actually uses this alignment practically: they get correspondences between animal brain neural recordings and AI model internal representations. To Hodak, this wasn't just interesting — it was a clue that AI was "on the right track" and that "there's some fact about the universe where these things, as they're learning, are grabbing onto some true underlying data manifold."

Hodak 的世界观中还有一个独特之处：柏拉图式表征假说——AI 模型和生物大脑在概念表征上趋向于同一个底层结构。"当你深入研究这些大型 AI 模型时，看到的数学结构和神经科学中的东西非常相似。" Science 实际上已经在实践中利用了这种对齐：他们找到了动物神经记录与 AI 模型内部表征之间的对应关系。对 Hodak 来说，这不只是有趣，而是 AI 走在正确道路上的证据——"宇宙中存在某种规律，这些系统在学习过程中，正在抓住某种真实的底层数据流形。"

The long game is substrate independence — the ability to move the brain's function off its biological substrate entirely. Hodak frames it with characteristic directness: "The only organ you can't even in principle transplant is the brain." Heart, pancreas, liver, lungs — "they're really support characters. They're there to keep the brain activity interesting and going." The goal is not just extending lifespan but eliminating the fragility: "I'm going to be ultimately fairly disappointed if I'm murdered by my pancreas."

Hodak 的长期目标是"基底独立性"——让大脑功能摆脱对生物基底的依赖。他的表达一如既往地直接："唯一在原则上无法移植的器官就是大脑。" 心脏、胰腺、肝脏、肺——"它们其实都是配角，存在的意义是让大脑保持有趣的活动。" 目标不只是延长寿命，而是消除这种脆弱性："如果最终被我的胰腺杀死，我会相当失望的。"
