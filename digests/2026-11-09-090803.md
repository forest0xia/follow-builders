### Box CEO Aaron Levie

Aaron Levie laid out why the shift from chat-based AI to agent swarms represents a step-change, not a linear upgrade. We're moving from AI that generates outputs in a conversational loop to systems where you set a problem for agents to work on in parallel — removing the upper bound on productivity gains that has capped AI-assisted work at 20–50%. Levie singles out cybersecurity and life sciences as early beneficiaries of this fan-out model. He also argues that now — with six months of compounding gains in computer use, reasoning, and domain expertise — is precisely when you should retry whatever AI use case didn't work before.

### Nan Yu, product at Codex/OpenAI

Nan Yu (product at Codex, formerly head of product at Linear) made a sharp case for starting with the "dumbest" architecture rather than the "simplest" one. His reasoning: simple is a destination you iterate toward, but dumb is the correct starting point — trying to be clever early means you outsmart yourself before the problem is clear. He also made a broader point that cuts to the heart of the AI-assisted era: software is easy now when you know what you want to build, and much harder when you don't. Without clarity, you'll ship junk at speed — and unlike the pre-AI era where nothing shipped, that's actually worse.
https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2119462979179892925
https://x.com/thenanyu/status/2119462224721002560

Nan Yu（Codex/OpenAI 产品，前 Linear 产品负责人）提出了一个犀利论点：应该从"最蠢"的架构出发，而不是"最简单"的架构。他的逻辑是：简洁是迭代后到达的终点，而"蠢"才是正确的起点——过早追求聪明，会在问题还没搞清楚之前就把自己绕进去。他还提出了一个直击 AI 辅助时代核心的观点：当你清楚自己要构建什么时，软件开发变得很容易；而当你不清楚时，则更难了。缺乏清晰度，你会以更快的速度交付垃圾——与 AI 之前那个什么都没有的时代相比，这反而更糟。

### Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch shipped an unusually detailed technical demo: a physical simulation of a record player built with WebGL2 and Web Audio. The simulation works by converting a song into the actual groove shape it would cut into vinyl, then running a virtual stylus across those groove walls 384,000 times per second — generating the crackle, pops, and inner-groove distortion as natural physical byproducts rather than audio effects. The code is open and remixable.
https://x.com/rauchg/status/2119536313712230802

### Latent Space: [One Million AI Engineer Special] How Stanford Teaches AI-Native Engineering — Mihail Eric, Monaco

**The Takeaway:** The generation entering the workforce now will be the first truly AI-native engineers — and the floor to being a valuable one has never been higher.

Mihail Eric is Adjunct Professor at Stanford (teaching CS 146S, the Modern Software Developer) and Head of AI at Monaco, an AI-native sales platform. He's one of the few people simultaneously building production AI systems and teaching the next generation of engineers how to do the same.

His curriculum reads like a practitioner's field guide: start with agent internals (build a Claude clone in 200 lines to understand what's actually happening), move through context engineering at scale, tool and skill design, agent-ready codebases, agentic code review, security, background agents, AI-native team structure, and finally the software factory. Each week builds toward the thesis that the factory is the destination — but you can only get there if the foundations are right.

The contrarian thread running through the course: most organizations are nowhere near factory-ready, and that's not a model problem. It's a codebase readiness problem. Eric borrows from Factory's work to push students toward understanding agent-ready design first — verifiability, legibility, structured surface boundaries — before even thinking about parallelization. "When I talk to companies of a certain scale, the things holding them back are really more just low-level DevOps, CI/CD stuff. Their merge queues are getting backed up. Throwing in one more SRE bot wouldn't be the answer."

On the student anxiety question — "should I study CS right now?" — his answer cuts through the noise: a well-trained AI-native engineer is among the most useful assets any company has right now. The floor is higher, not lower. First-year engineers need to show up closer to senior level. The ones who go through AI "psychosis" and come out the other side — who've shipped enough to know the difference between productive and delusional — are the ones who'll be valuable.

**要点：** 现在进入职场的这一代人将是第一批真正意义上的 AI-native 工程师——而成为一名有价值的 AI 工程师，门槛也从未如此之高。

Mihail Eric 是斯坦福大学兼职教授（讲授 CS 146S《现代软件开发者》课程），同时也是 AI-native 销售平台 Monaco 的 AI 负责人。他是极少数同时在生产环境中构建 AI 系统、并教导下一代工程师如何做到同样事情的人之一。
