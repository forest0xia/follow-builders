### Training Data — "Box's Aaron Levie: On Reinventing Yourself in the AI Age and Enterprise Diffusion"

**The Takeaway:** The gap between AI model capabilities and enterprise workflows is a trillion-dollar opportunity — and the companies that patiently bridge that gap will win the AI era.

Box CEO Aaron Levie has spent twenty years in enterprise content management, which gave him an unusual vantage point when AI took off: he could see exactly where the technology would get stuck. His core argument is that the "LLM wrapper" critique misses the point. In real enterprises, models need to connect to legacy data systems, navigate complex permission structures, handle human-in-the-loop moments, and fit into workflows that haven't changed in decades. None of that is a model problem — it's an application layer problem, and it represents an enormous opportunity.

Levie draws a parallel to cloud infrastructure: AWS and GCP created trillions in value, but Snowflake and Databricks also emerged because infrastructure alone wasn't enough. "The models will be insanely valuable, but the application of bringing those models into real workflows in banking and life sciences and healthcare and government — that's just going to be a lot of software."

On the AI content quality question (what he calls "work slop"), Levie's take is nuanced. Code's value is almost entirely represented in the text it produces — it's a utility, so AI-generated code is accepted, even preferred. But when someone reads a memo or a strategy deck, they're using it as a proxy for the person's judgment and commitment. That's why AI-generated business content still triggers an "allergic reaction" — the question isn't quality, it's trust.

On diffusion timelines, he's blunt: Silicon Valley is wildly underestimating how long enterprise AI adoption will take. Unlike coding agents — where the data lives in GitHub, the users are technical, and the feedback loop is instant — knowledge work requires accessing legacy file shares, navigating permission systems, and running change management across thousands of non-technical employees. "We have to prepare for diffusion taking a lot longer than they think."

His prediction for five years out: "90% of all tokens in the enterprise are things a user never kicked off. They just see a result."

> "You're either a year ahead or a year behind simply based on your feed."

**核心观点：** AI 模型能力与企业工作流之间的鸿沟，是一个价值万亿美元的机会——而那些有耐心弥合这道鸿沟的公司，将赢得整个 AI 时代。

Box CEO Aaron Levie 在企业内容管理领域深耕二十年，这让他在 AI 爆发时获得了一个独特的观察视角：他能清楚看到这项技术会在哪里卡壳。他的核心判断是："LLM 套壳"的批评没有抓住要点。在真实的企业中，模型需要连接遗留数据系统、处理复杂的权限结构、应对人工介入的环节，并融入几十年未变的工作流程。这些都不是模型问题——而是应用层问题，也是巨大的机会所在。

Levie 将此与云计算基础设施做类比：AWS 和 GCP 创造了万亿市值，但 Snowflake 和 Databricks 也随之崛起，因为光有基础设施还不够。"模型本身价值无穷，但把这些模型真正落地到银行、生命科学、医疗、政府机构的实际工作流程中——那将需要大量的软件。"

在 AI 内容质量问题（他称之为"work slop"）上，Levie 给出了细腻的分析。代码的价值几乎完全体现在它生成的文本里——它是一种工具，所以 AI 写代码不仅被接受，甚至更受欢迎。但当有人阅读一份备忘录或战略文档时，他们把它当作对方判断力和投入程度的替代指标。这就是为什么 AI 生成的商业内容仍然会触发"过敏反应"——问题不在于质量，而在于信任。

在扩散时间线问题上，他直言不讳：硅谷严重低估了企业 AI 落地所需的时间。与编程 agent 不同——后者的数据在 GitHub、用户是技术人员、反馈循环即时——知识工作需要访问遗留文件共享、处理权限体系、并在数以千计的非技术员工中推进变革管理。"我们必须做好准备：扩散会比大家想象的慢得多。"

他对五年后的预测："90% 的企业 token 消耗，都来自用户从未主动触发的任务。他们只是看到一个结果。"

> "你的信息茧房决定你是领先一年，还是落后一年。"
