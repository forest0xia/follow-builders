**AI & I by Every — "The Founder of a $1.5B AI Company on What Comes After the First Wave of AI Apps"**

**The Takeaway:** Meeting notes were never the endgame — the real prize is inventing how humans and AI will work together, and no one has cracked it yet.

Granola CEO Chris Pedregal built a $1.5B AI meeting company while believing from the start that meeting notes "are not the end all, be all value that everyone's running after." When Notion, OpenAI, and Zoom all launched their own AI meeting tools, Granola's growth rate barely moved. The reason: the future is too expansive for any one feature to define it.

In a conversation with Every CEO Dan Shipper, both founders traced the emerging shape of AI-native work. Shipper described work bifurcating into two surfaces: async delegation via Slack bots (multiplayer, proactive, shareable), and deep single-player collaboration in Codex or Claude desktop. He called apps designed for this second mode "Codex-native": they expose a CLI alongside a web UI so the agent and user can both manipulate the same state at the same time while looking at the same interface. He now does his email, writing, and even internet provider switching this way.

Pedregal's key product insight: humans won't wait 20 seconds for an agent mid-meeting. So Granola pre-generates millions of pre-meeting briefs — even if only 10% are ever opened — because the value spikes precisely when you need it most: two minutes before you're late to a meeting and can't remember who you're talking to. He describes the ideal product as a "handrail": invisible most of the time, but load-bearing exactly when you trip.

On token economics: Fable's release prompted both companies to finally start tracking internal AI usage. At Every, one team member was on pace to spend a million dollars in model credits before anyone noticed.

"I think we are in the very, very early steps of a computing revolution. What has come thus far will pale in comparison to what will come soon."

**AI & I by Every — "The Founder of a $1.5B AI Company on What Comes After the First Wave of AI Apps"**

**核心观点：** 会议记录从来都不是终局——真正的机会在于重新发明人类与 AI 的协作方式，而这个问题至今无人解决。

Granola CEO Chris Pedregal 将一家 AI 会议工具公司带到了 15 亿美元的估值，但他从一开始就清楚，"会议记录并不是所有人都在追逐的终极价值。" 后来 Notion、OpenAI 和 Zoom 相继推出自家的 AI 会议工具，Granola 的增长率却几乎没有受到影响。原因在于：未来的格局太大，没有哪一个功能能定义它。

在与 Every CEO Dan Shipper 的对话中，两位创始人描绘了 AI 原生工作方式的雏形。Shipper 认为工作正在分化为两个界面：通过 Slack bot 进行异步委托（多人协作、主动触发、可共享），以及在 Codex 或 Claude desktop 中进行深度单人协作。他将为后一种模式设计的应用称为"Codex 原生应用"：这类应用同时提供 CLI 和 Web 界面，让 agent 和用户能同时操作同一份状态，看到同一个界面。他本人已经用这种方式处理邮件、写作，甚至换网络运营商。

Pedregal 的核心产品洞察：用户在会议中不会等待 agent 20 秒的响应。所以 Granola 预先生成了数百万份会前简报——即使只有 10% 会被打开——因为真正需要它的时刻价值极高：就是你会议迟到两分钟、完全不记得要见谁的那一刻。他将理想产品形容为"扶手"：平时隐形，但在你需要它的那一刻，它必须承得住力。

关于 token 支出：Fable 发布之后，两家公司才开始认真追踪内部 AI 使用量。在 Every，有一位团队成员的模型调用费用已经在向百万美元飞奔，无人事先察觉。

"我认为我们正处于一场计算革命最最早期的步骤中。迄今为止所发生的一切，将在即将到来的事物面前显得微不足道。"
