**AI & I by Every — "The Inside Story of the 50 Who Built Modern AI With Kevin Roose"**

**The Takeaway:** Kevin Roose, journalist and author of "AGI Chronicles," argues that how we decide to use AI — not the technology itself — will define what this era becomes. His "weightlifting vs. forklifting" framework cuts through the AI productivity debate more cleanly than almost anything else in circulation.

Kevin Roose spent two years interviewing 150 insiders — founders, researchers, and operators at OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind — to write the book none of the AI labs wanted him to write. He went in background-only, trading anonymity for candor, and the result is what he describes as the canonical account of how AGI arrived. The book became a New York Times bestseller, helped by the accelerating chaos of the AI moment it chronicles.

His most memorable frame is "weightlifting vs. forklifting." Weightlifting tasks are those where struggle produces the benefit — parenting, serious writing, creative work you care about. Using AI for them doesn't just fail to help; it robs you of the point. Forklifting tasks are those where you're just trying to move something heavy from A to B. For those, the tool is the right answer. "It would be a total waste of time to bring a forklift into the gym and have it lift weights for you," he said. "By the same token, I don't think it makes a lot of sense to swear off forklifts."

Roose used AI extensively while writing the book — just not for the writing itself. He built a "council of Claudes" via Claude Code: a swarm of agents each assigned a different editorial persona (AI skeptic, AI historian, Isaacson-style craftsperson), synthesized by a master editor agent. It caught factual errors his human editors missed. He also relied on NotebookLM to query across 150 interviews' worth of notes. "A couple years ago, we had to fact check the AIs," he said. "And now the AIs fact check me."

On regulation, Roose is pragmatic: absent federal AI rules under the current administration, the first necessary step is an industry certification standard — requiring safety testing before model releases, the same way pharmaceuticals must be tested before hitting the market. He also believes the AI race is finally moving fast enough that leading figures like Dario Amodei, Demis Hassabis, and Sam Altman genuinely want to slow down — to buy safety teams time to develop new monitoring and control mechanisms.

His new podcast, Machine Gods, launches with Casey Newton on NPR, covering AI as the most important story in the world — two episodes per week.
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

**要点：** Kevin Roose 是记者，也是《AGI Chronicles》的作者。他认为，定义这个时代的不是技术本身，而是我们如何选择使用 AI。他的「举铁 vs. 叉车」框架，是当下 AI 生产力辩论中最清晰的思考工具之一。

Kevin Roose 花了两年时间采访 150 位 OpenAI、Anthropic 和 DeepMind 的内部人士——包括创始人、研究员和运营者——写出了这本各大 AI 实验室都不希望他写的书。他以匿名换取坦诚，全程以背景资料方式进行采访，最终写成了他所描述的"AGI 诞生史的权威记录"。这本书登上《纽约时报》畅销书榜，受益于它所记录的 AI 时代本身的加速混乱。

他最令人印象深刻的框架是「举铁 vs. 叉车」。举铁型任务，是那些"挣扎本身就是价值所在"的事——养育孩子、认真写作、你真正在乎的创作。用 AI 代劳不仅帮不上忙，更剥夺了意义。叉车型任务，是那些只需要把重物从 A 搬到 B 的事。对于这些，工具就是正确答案。"把叉车开进健身房帮你举铁，完全是在浪费时间，"他说，"同样，发誓不用叉车也没有意义。"

Roose 在写书过程中大量使用了 AI——只是没有用于写作本身。他通过 Claude Code 搭建了一个"Claude 顾问团"：一组各自扮演不同编辑角色的 agent（AI 怀疑论者、AI 历史学家、Isaacson 风格的工艺师），由一个主编辑 agent 综合汇总。这个系统发现了他和人类编辑都没发现的事实性错误。他还借助 NotebookLM 在 150 份采访笔记中检索。"几年前，我们还得帮 AI 核实事实，"他说，"现在，AI 在帮我核实事实了。"

在监管问题上，Roose 态度务实：在当前政府缺乏联邦 AI 法规的情况下，第一步必须建立行业认证标准——要求模型发布前进行安全测试，就像药品上市前必须经过测试一样。他也相信，AI 竞赛已经快到连 Dario Amodei、Demis Hassabis 和 Sam Altman 这样的领军人物都真心希望放慢脚步——为安全团队争取时间，开发新的监控和控制机制。
