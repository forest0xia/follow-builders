**AI & I by Every — "A New Vision for Human Work After Automation"**

**The Takeaway:** In a world where AI beats human experts 83% of the time, your unique value isn't what you know — it's the irreducible perspective you bring to the model.

Every co-founder and CEO Dan Shipper delivered the closing talk at Every's first conference (V.C.S.S.) in Brooklyn, taking on the question that defined the whole event: *What does great human work look like after automation?* He came armed with a striking demonstration: in one week, using Claude, he did the work of a data scientist, game developer, marketer, engineer, and salesperson — tasks that would have cost roughly $200K in pre-AI terms — all within the cracks of his existing calendar.

The aggregate data is brutal. OpenAI's GDP-Val benchmark shows AI beating human experts 83% of the time, up from 12.4% in 2024. Shipper's insight is that looking only at the aggregate will make you despair — and also make you miss what's really happening.

His reframe: when every person on a team has access to the same model, the default AI output becomes cheap and undifferentiated. What's valuable — the "alpha" — is how each person uses the model through the lens of their own responsibility and perspective. His team proved it: given the same survey data and the same Claude, their go-to-market engineer found churn caused by email volume, their marketing head found reader loneliness, and their senior editor made a Claymation game show. Claude's default answer? Generic insight "anyone could get."

"Generation is really cheap, but ranking is hard." Claude can predict what Shipper would say in Slack 93% of the time — but only if it generates 400 candidates for you to pick from. It cannot reliably rank the right one to the top position. That gap is where humans live.

His conclusion, which he calls the **allocation economy**: you won't be judged on how much you know, but on how well you allocate and manage the resources of intelligence. As model capabilities become commoditized and distributed to everyone, the floor keeps rising — but so do the people using the model with their own perspective. The real comparison isn't you vs. 2027 Claude; it's you-with-2027-Claude vs. default-prompt-of-2027-Claude. Shipper picks Yash — the human with perspective and responsibility — every single time.
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WstNofgQF5Q

**重点一句话：** 在 AI 以 83% 的胜率超越人类专家的世界里，你的独特价值不在于你知道什么，而在于你带给模型的那个不可替代的视角。

Every 联合创始人兼 CEO Dan Shipper 在 Every 首届大会（V.C.S.S.）的闭幕演讲中，正面回应了贯穿全天的核心问题：*自动化之后，出色的人类工作究竟是什么样的？* 他用一个有力的案例开场：在一周时间里，他用 Claude 完成了数据科学家、游戏开发者、市场营销、工程师和销售人员的工作——在 AI 出现之前，这些工作加起来大约价值 20 万美元——而这一切都是在日常日历的缝隙中完成的。

宏观数据已经很残酷：OpenAI 的 GDP-Val 基准测试显示，AI 在专家工作上以 83% 的胜率超越人类（2024 年这一数字还是 12.4%）。Shipper 的洞察在于：只看宏观数据会让你陷入绝望，也会让你错过真正正在发生的事。

他的重新框架是：当团队里每个人都能用同一个模型时，AI 的默认输出就变得廉价且同质化。真正有价值的"alpha"，是每个人如何通过自己的职责和视角来驱动模型。他的团队证明了这一点：面对同样的调研数据和同一个 Claude，增长工程师发现了邮件过多导致的流失问题，市场负责人发现了读者的孤独感，高级编辑则做了一个黏土定格动画《问答游戏》。而 Claude 的默认回答？"任何人都能得到的"通用洞察。

"生成很便宜，但排序很难。" Claude 能以 93% 的概率预测 Shipper 在 Slack 上会说什么——但前提是它能生成 400 个候选答案让你来选。它无法可靠地把正确答案排到第一位。人类价值就存在于这个差距之中。

他的结论，也就是他所称的**分配经济**：你不会因为知道多少而被评判，而是因为你能多好地分配和管理智能资源。随着模型能力不断商品化并向所有人普及，底线在不断提高——但积极用自己视角使用模型的人也在同步提升。真正的对比不是你 vs. 2027 年的 Claude，而是你+2027 年 Claude vs. 2027 年 Claude 的默认 prompt。Shipper 每次都会选择 Yash——那个有视角和责任感的人。
