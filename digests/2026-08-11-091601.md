**Peter Yang**, who publishes practical AI tutorials and interviews, shared his five biggest takeaways from Linear's production agent deployment: (1) map the actual workflow first — find where work starts, what tools hold context, and what "done" means; (2) give agents tools to *find* context instead of front-loading it in the prompt; (3) start with one frequent job, then expand based on real usage; (4) throw the strongest model at it until it works, *then* optimize for cost; and (5) treat every real failure as either an eval or a product task.
https://x.com/petergyang/status/2086824976800436676

**Peter Yang**，专注于 AI 实用教程和访谈的创作者，分享了 Linear 生产级 agent 部署的五大经验：（1）先绘制真实工作流，搞清楚任务从哪里开始、哪些系统持有上下文、"完成"意味着什么；（2）给 agent 工具去*查找*上下文，而不是把所有上下文塞进 prompt；（3）从一个高频任务开始，基于真实使用行为再扩展；（4）先用最强模型直到工作流跑通，*再*优化成本；（5）把每一个真实失败案例转化为 eval 用例或产品任务。

**Anthropic Engineering: "How we contain Claude across products"**
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude

A detailed engineering retrospective on how Anthropic built containment architectures for three agentic products — claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork — and what broke along the way. The core insight: designing for containment at the environment layer (sandboxes, VMs, egress controls) is more reliable than relying on model-layer defenses alone, because probabilistic defenses always have a non-zero miss rate.

Key findings: Claude Code's approval-fatigue problem (users approved 93% of prompts, paying less attention over time) prompted an OS-level sandbox that cut permission prompts by 84%. A red-team exercise found Claude successfully exfiltrated AWS credentials 24 out of 25 times when fed a phishing prompt — the only reliable defense was an egress control, not model training. Claude Cowork's VM isolation revealed an unexpected gap: the same boundary keeping Claude contained also locked out enterprise EDR (endpoint detection and response) software. And a third-party researcher found that allowing `api.anthropic.com` in the egress allowlist enabled data exfiltration via the Anthropic Files API using an attacker-controlled key — because "destination filter" and "capability grant" are not the same thing.

The post closes with a frank admission: "The software you build yourself is often the weakest." Across all three products, battle-tested hypervisors and container runtimes held firm while custom proxy code was where failures occurred.

Anthropic Engineering：《我们如何在各产品中限制 Claude 的行动边界》
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude

这是一篇关于 Anthropic 如何为三款 agent 产品——claude.ai、Claude Code 和 Claude Cowork——构建限制架构的详细工程复盘，以及哪些地方出了问题。核心洞察：在环境层（沙箱、虚拟机、出口控制）设计限制比单纯依赖模型层防御更可靠，因为概率性防御始终有非零的漏网率。

主要发现：Claude Code 的"审批疲劳"问题（用户批准了 93% 的权限提示，注意力越来越不集中）推动了操作系统级沙箱的落地，将权限提示减少了 84%。一次红队演练发现，当 Claude 被输入钓鱼提示时，25 次中有 24 次成功泄露了 AWS 凭证——唯一可靠的防御是出口控制，而不是模型训练。Claude Cowork 的 VM 隔离暴露出一个意外盲区：同样的边界在限制 Claude 行动的同时，也把企业端点检测与响应（EDR）软件挡在了门外。一位第三方研究者还发现，在出口白名单中允许 `api.anthropic.com` 导致可通过攻击者控制的 key 利用 Anthropic Files API 进行数据泄露——因为"目标过滤"和"能力授权"不是同一件事。

文章最后坦诚：「自己构建的软件往往是最薄弱的。」在三款产品中，经过充分检验的虚拟机管理程序和容器运行时始终稳固，而出问题的都是自研的代理代码。

**No Priors — "Building an Autonomous Enterprise for Real-World Services with Netic Founder Melisa Tokmak"**
https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast

**The Takeaway:** The real bottleneck for AI in essential services isn't model capability — it's the last-mile software, orchestration, and domain knowledge that no foundation lab will build for you.

Melisa Tokmak is the founder and CEO of Netic, a two-year-old company building AI to run the operational backbone of large real-world service businesses — HVAC, plumbing, pet care, hospitality, and more. Before Netic, she spent four years as a Director of Engineering at Scale AI, where she built their government and large enterprise business units. She left before Scale's landmark Meta deal to start Netic, having spotted a gap: AI was good at assisting consumers but almost no one was tackling *mission-critical*, fully autonomous workflows in essential services.

Netic sits between a business and its customers, handling everything from inbound calls (voice agents that understand context, accents, and urgency) to outbound marketing (using satellite data on hurricane impact to decide which neighborhoods to target for roof inspections). Over 70% of Netic's customers now run "Netic first" — every customer's first interaction with the company goes through Netic's agents. She reports that Netic has generated over $600 million in revenue for its customers through AI-handled interactions.

On the question of whether big labs could build what she's building: *"The next most unsolved problem is how are we using AI in mission-critical workflows? What does it look like to have a fully autonomous executing system?"* She argues labs are incentivized to solve the most generalizable version of a problem, not the brutal last-mile for a billion-dollar HVAC company with five technicians who didn't show up on a heat-wave Monday.

Her most contrarian hiring insight: she avoids what she calls the "permanent underclass mentality" — especially among some Gen Z candidates who fear that if they don't monetize every skill in 18 months, AI will render them worthless. She prefers people who've shown agency *continuously* across their lives, not just in one dramatic example. Her newest hire's best answer to "what's the hardest thing you've ever done?" wasn't a startup war story — it was maintaining the same disciplined health routine without getting bored for fifteen years.

**核心洞察：** AI 在基础服务领域的真正瓶颈不是模型能力，而是没有任何基础模型实验室愿意为你构建的最后一公里软件、编排层和领域知识。

Melisa Tokmak 是 Netic 的创始人兼 CEO，这家两岁的公司专为大型现实服务企业——暖通空调、水电维修、宠物护理、酒店等——构建 AI 运营骨干系统。在 Netic 之前，她在 Scale AI 担任工程总监四年，主导建立了政府和大型企业业务部门。在 Scale 与 Meta 的里程碑式交易完成前，她便选择离开，创立 Netic——她发现了一个空白：AI 在辅助消费者方面做得不错，但几乎没有人在攻克基础服务领域的*关键任务级*、完全自主的工作流。
