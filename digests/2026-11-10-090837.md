**Latent Space: What Frontier Labs Buy: A Data Deep Dive with Handshake AI — Garrett Lord & Team**

**The Takeaway:** Training AI at the frontier now requires genuine domain expertise — not just college students drawing boxes around objects — and building the environments where agents learn might be the most valuable and underappreciated skill in AI right now.

Handshake AI (the company Handshake pivoted toward) is in the business of supplying the data that frontier labs need to train their most capable models. Garrett Lord and his team joined Latent Space to get specific about what that actually means in 2026.

The story of data quality over the past three years tracks closely with the capabilities curve. Early language models were producing what Garrett calls "gibberish," and college students could usefully rank outputs for RLHF. That era is over. Now, the work involves building what Handshake calls *simulated worlds* — realistic RL environments that model what an agent would encounter inside a bank, law firm, or hospital. The person authoring that world needs to understand it deeply enough to make it both complex and realistic. And the hardest part isn't even building the world: it's defining what "good" looks like for tasks where most knowledge work isn't perfectly verifiable.

One counterintuitive point from Garrett: you don't necessarily need a world-class professor for this. Many tasks where models still fail can be addressed by a skilled two-year investment banker. The expertise bar has risen sharply from 2022, but there's still a wide range of productive work that doesn't require the absolute frontier of human knowledge.

The market numbers tell the story: three years ago this was a billion-dollar market. It's now $10 billion. In three more years, Garrett sees no scenario where it isn't a hundred billion. The bottleneck isn't demand — it's the supply of people who can author these environments well enough to matter.

> "If it's ninety-five percent right, it's a hundred percent wrong — because it takes just as much time, if not more, to fix a flawed output as it is to just build from scratch or use a template."

**Latent Space：前沿实验室在购买什么——与 Handshake AI（Garrett Lord 及团队）深度探讨数据**

**核心结论：** 训练前沿 AI 模型如今需要真正的领域专业知识，而非仅靠标注图片框的大学生——而如何构建 agent 的学习环境，或许是当下 AI 领域最有价值、也最被低估的能力。

Handshake AI（由 Handshake 转型而来的公司）专门向前沿实验室提供训练最强模型所需的数据。Garrett Lord 和他的团队参加了 Latent Space，深入剖析了这在 2026 年究竟意味着什么。

过去三年，数据质量的演变与模型能力的提升高度同步。早期语言模型输出的内容 Garrett 称之为"乱码"，大学生足以胜任 RLHF 的标注工作。那个时代已经过去。如今的工作是构建 Handshake 所称的"模拟世界"——逼真的 RL 环境，模拟 agent 在银行、律所或医院中实际遭遇的场景。搭建这个世界的人需要对其有深度理解，才能同时做到复杂且真实。而最难的部分甚至不是搭建环境本身：而是定义什么是"好的结果"——因为大多数知识工作并不存在可以精确验证的标准答案。

Garrett 有一个反直觉的观点：你并不一定需要顶级教授来做这件事。许多模型仍然会出错的任务，一位有两年经验的技术型投行人员完全可以胜任。自 2022 年以来，专业门槛确实大幅提高，但仍有大量有效工作不需要触及人类知识的绝对前沿。

数字说明一切：三年前这是一个十亿美元的市场，如今已是百亿级别。再过三年，Garrett 看不到它不达到千亿规模的可能性。瓶颈不在需求，而在于能够高质量构建这些环境的人才供给。

> "如果它 95% 是对的，那它就是 100% 错的——因为修复一个有缺陷的输出，所花费的时间和从头做起或使用模板相比，丝毫不会更少，甚至更多。"
