**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 94: Applied Compute CEO on the Limits of RL, the New AI Hyperscaler & Why Post-Training Wins Inference**

The Takeaway: Post-training is the most underinvested layer of the AI stack, and owning model infrastructure — not just swapping API providers — is the real competitive moat.

Applied Compute CEO Josh (formerly at OpenAI) makes the case for why every serious AI company will eventually need to post-train its own models. The argument isn't that labs are malicious — it's about flexibility, control, and avoiding vendor lock-in as labs increasingly compete with their own customers. "The safest and most durable thing you can do is invest in the full stack to be able to make models that are powering your products."

On RL's limits: RL is a "hill-climbing machine," and the hardest part isn't the climb — it's defining the hill. For that reason, Applied Compute views evals as among the most competitively sensitive assets a company can own. "Your employees are not fungible. You would not be comfortable with them going to another company and doing work there. Same thing with these models." Don't share your evals with labs, even if it might accelerate model improvement.

On when to post-train: Start with harness and context optimization. Once you've squeezed most of the gains there, post-training the model's policy — how it makes judgments, how it uses tools — can unlock additional value that prompt engineering alone cannot. The best signal for whether to post-train: do you have out-of-distribution data unique to your business?

On inference: Post-training wins inference too. The most at-scale workloads — where you're spending the most on tokens — are where post-training returns are greatest. Applied Compute co-optimizes training and inference together, adjusting disaggregated prefill/decode setups based on how a model was trained.

The big vision: Applied Compute sees itself building the new AI hyperscaler. Just as AWS and Azure turned CPU compute into flexible application infrastructure, the company aims to do the same for GPU compute and models — starting from the hardest, most defensible layer (training) and building upward to inference, routing, and beyond.
https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

**要点：** Post-training 是 AI 技术栈中投入最不足的一层，真正的竞争护城河在于拥有模型基础设施，而不仅仅是替换 API 提供商。

Applied Compute CEO Josh（前 OpenAI 员工）阐述了为什么所有认真做 AI 的公司最终都需要进行模型 post-training。这不是说各大 AI 实验室心怀恶意——而是关乎灵活性、控制权，以及在各实验室日益与自己的客户形成竞争时避免被绑定在单一供应商上。"你能做的最安全、最持久的事，就是投资完整的技术栈，打造驱动你产品的专属模型。"

关于 RL 的局限：RL 本质上是一台"爬山机"，最难的不是爬山本身，而是定义那座山。因此 Applied Compute 认为 eval（评估基准）是公司最具竞争敏感性的资产之一。"你的员工不可以随意替换——你不会希望他们跳槽去竞争对手那边继续工作。对 AI 模型也是同样的道理。" 不要把你的 evals 分享给各大 AI 实验室，即便这样做可能加速模型改进。

关于何时进行 post-training：先从 harness 和上下文优化入手，把这两方面的收益榨干之后，再对模型的 policy 进行 post-training——即模型如何做判断、如何使用工具——这能解锁提示工程无法实现的额外价值。判断是否应该 post-training 的最佳信号：你是否拥有你业务独有的分布外（out-of-distribution）数据？

关于推理：Post-training 同样赢得了推理端。消耗 token 最多的大规模工作负载，正是 post-training 回报最高的场景。Applied Compute 将训练与推理联合优化，甚至会根据模型训练方式来调整 prefill/decode 的拆分部署策略。

宏大愿景：Applied Compute 将自己定位为下一代 AI 超大规模基础设施服务商。正如 AWS 和 Azure 将 CPU 算力转化为灵活的应用基础设施，Applied Compute 也希望对 GPU 算力和模型做同样的事——从最难、最有壁垒的层（训练）出发，向上延伸到推理、路由及更多领域。
