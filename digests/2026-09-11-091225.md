**Meta AI 高级总监（前谷歌 Gemini、Veo、Nano 负责人）Madhu Guru** 在其"如何构建优秀 Evals"系列中更新了第 10 篇：衡量过程步骤，而不只是最终结果。他以高中数学打比方——答对了还不够，解题过程同样重要。两个 agent 的轨迹可能产出相同答案（"42！"），但一个只用了 4 次干净的工具调用，另一个则调用了 17 次、重复搜索 3 次、并从 2 次报错中恢复。轨迹质量与最终精度同等重要。

**Aaron Levie**, CEO of Box, returned from a week of meetings with dozens of technology leaders across banking, media, insurance, information services, and consulting. His field report covers seven enterprise AI themes: (1) Cybersecurity anxiety is at a peak — the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident put everyone on edge. (2) Model wars continue — enterprises are deploying multiple frontier models, with dollars concentrated on a few vendors and open-weight models still nascent at scale. (3) Agent identity and security are emerging concerns, especially managing what agents can access and when they need to act as the user. (4) Process reengineering — the real ROI comes when agents change the workflow itself, not just layer on top. (5) Frequent architecture swaps — "we tried X and moved to Y" is now the norm because vendors who fall behind get replaced fast. (6) Evals remain immature — few enterprises have a solid grasp of how to measure their AI workflows. (7) Legacy systems are still the #1 drag on adoption. He also announced Box's deeper partnership with OpenAI to let enterprise users work with Box content securely inside ChatGPT.

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck** — *When AI Improves Itself | Richard Socher (Recursive)*

**The Takeaway:** Recursive self-improvement — AI that builds better AI — may be the most consequential unlock in scientific history, but it requires first confronting why human scientific progress has fundamentally slowed.

Matt Turck sits down with Richard Socher, one of the most cited AI researchers alive and founder of Recursive, which just raised $650M to build AI systems that improve themselves. Socher opens with a counterintuitive premise: scientific progress is slowing. Not because we lack effort or money, but because human knowledge has fractured into thousands of specialized subfields that are nearly impossible for any single person — or even group — to synthesize. We solved bacterial infections with antibiotics; we have not come close to solving cancer or viruses at the same level. Foundational breakthroughs like E=MC² and general relativity haven't been matched in decades. The reason, Socher argues, is that the knowledge labyrinth has become so dense — 34,000 journals with "no trespassing" signs — that even the most brilliant researchers can't bridge disciplines fast enough.

This is where AI changes everything. Socher believes that anything you can simulate, AI will solve. Large language models, by learning from vast cross-domain text, can become the first synthetic minds capable of holding the entire labyrinth in their heads — and finding the cross-disciplinary connections humans miss. His company Recursive is building AI that doesn't just assist researchers, but itself generates and tests hypotheses, entering recursive loops of self-improvement. As verifiers improve (systems that can tell whether a scientific claim is true), the ceiling on AI's ability to drive discovery rises rapidly. Socher is blunt: we are nowhere near the upper bounds of AI intelligence, and the gap between where we are and where we could be is staggering. His new book, *The Eureka Machine*, is a blueprint for what this future looks like.
https://www.youtube.com/@DataDrivenNYC/videos

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck** — *《当 AI 自我改进时》| Richard Socher（Recursive）*

**核心洞察：** 递归自我改进——AI 构建更好的 AI——可能是科学史上最具决定性意义的突破，但前提是我们首先要直面人类科学进步为何从根本上放缓。

Matt Turck 与被引用次数最多的 AI 研究员之一、Recursive 创始人 Richard Socher 展开深度对话。Recursive 刚刚完成 6.5 亿美元融资，致力于构建能够自我改进的 AI 系统。Socher 以一个反直觉的论点开场：科学进步正在放缓。这并非因为缺乏努力或资金，而是因为人类知识已碎片化为数千个高度专业化的子领域，任何个人——甚至任何团队——都几乎无法将其整合贯通。我们用抗生素解决了细菌感染；但在攻克癌症和病毒方面，我们远未达到同等水平。E=MC²、广义相对论这样的基础性突破已数十年未曾出现。Socher 认为，根本原因在于知识迷宫变得过于密集——34,000 本期刊如同一块块"禁止入内"的牌子——即使最聪明的研究者也无法以足够快的速度跨越学科边界。

这正是 AI 改变一切的地方。Socher 相信：凡是能够被模拟的，AI 终将解决。大型语言模型通过学习海量跨领域文本，有望成为第一批能够在脑海中容纳整个知识迷宫的合成智能体——并发现人类错失的跨学科关联。他的公司 Recursive 正在构建不只是辅助研究者、而是能自主生成和验证假说的 AI，进入递归自我改进的循环。随着验证器（能判断科学结论是否为真的系统）不断进化，AI 推动科学发现的上限将快速提升。Socher 直言：我们距离 AI 智能的上限还差得很远，我们现在所处的位置与我们能到达的位置之间，鸿沟令人震惊。他的新书《The Eureka Machine》正是为这一未来绘制了蓝图。
