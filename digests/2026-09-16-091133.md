**Thariq, Claude Code at Anthropic (prev YC W20, SPC, MIT Media Lab)**

Thariq made a notable architectural call: MCPs are better than CLIs for most integrations, and he wasn't expecting to land there. The key reason — models have gotten dramatically better at tool calling, MCP is now stateless, and you can defer tools. For any use case that involves composing or filtering data, his guidance is to add params like `query` directly to MCP tools. A quiet but important signal that the integration layer is consolidating around MCP.

### Training Data — "Box's Aaron Levie: On Reinventing Yourself in the AI Age and Enterprise Diffusion"

**The Takeaway:** The gap between what AI models can do and what enterprises have actually automated is enormous — and Aaron Levie thinks the companies that figure out how to close it will capture trillions of dollars. The ones that misread the moment will lose. It's already game over for them; they just don't know it yet.

Aaron Levie built Box on a bet that enterprises needed a better way to store, share, and collaborate on unstructured data in the cloud. Twenty years later, he's running the same company — but the AI moment has made it entirely different. When ChatGPT hit, Box went all in: a dedicated team, daily standups, the best people assigned to the problem. The use case became obvious fast: Box sits on hundreds of billions of files. Agents can now be deployed against that data to answer questions, extract structured metadata from contracts and research documents, and automate entire workflow steps — bank onboarding compressed from two weeks to an hour.

On the model race: Levie runs custom evals against every major model — a "complex work eval" with domain-specific knowledge work scenarios and a holdback eval using real Box employee data. His current read: Fable 5.1 is state of the art, Gemini outperforms on some enterprise use cases, and Grok/Muse/GPT-5-class models are genuinely neck and neck. On open weights: enterprise adoption is higher than people think, lower than enterprises actually want, and far below what it'll be in five years. His framework for how this plays out — borrowed from Decagon's Jesse — is that as each use case matures, workloads peel off to open-source models where economics or post-training gains make sense. "You'll have Fable for orchestration and an open weights model for long-tail tasks, and somehow everybody wins."

His clearest provocation on content vs. code: code is a utility — nobody cares who wrote it as long as it works. Content is different because it carries human intent, taste, and trust signals. "Work slop" isn't just a quality problem; it's an identity problem. The resistance to AI-generated content in the enterprise isn't irrational — it's a signal about what people think their work is supposed to mean.

His thesis for the applied AI era: "There is quite literally trillions up for grabs at the applied layer, and the companies that know how to build the teams and get to the enterprise will be the ones that win. It's just obviously guaranteed."

### Training Data — "Box's Aaron Levie: On Reinventing Yourself in the AI Age and Enterprise Diffusion"

**核心结论：** AI 模型的能力与企业实际已自动化的工作之间，存在巨大鸿沟——Aaron Levie 认为，能够弥合这个鸿沟的公司将能拿走数万亿美元。误判这一时刻的公司将出局，只是他们自己还不知道。

Aaron Levie 创建 Box，押注的是企业需要更好的方式在云端存储、共享和协作非结构化数据。二十年后，他还在经营同一家公司——但 AI 时代让这家公司变得截然不同。ChatGPT 出现时，Box 全力押注：组建专属团队，每日 standup，最优秀的人全押上。使用场景很快变得显而易见：Box 上存着数以千亿计的文件。Agent 现在可以被部署去查询这些数据、从合同和研究文档中提取结构化元数据，并将整个工作流步骤自动化——银行客户入驻流程从两周压缩到一小时。

关于模型竞争：Levie 用定制评测集评估每一个主流模型——一套"复杂工作评测"（涵盖不同行业的知识工作场景）和一套基于真实 Box 员工数据的保留评测集。他目前的判断是：Fable 5.1 是最强的，Gemini 在某些企业场景表现突出，而 Grok、Muse、GPT-5 级别的模型实力真的旗鼓相当。关于开源模型：企业采用率比人们想象的高，但远低于企业真正想要的，也远低于五年后的水平。他关于开源与闭源共存的框架——借鉴自 Decagon 的 Jesse——是：随着每个使用场景趋于成熟，工作负载会逐步剥离到开源模型上，在经济性或微调收益上占优。"你会用 Fable 做编排，用开源模型处理长尾任务，结果是所有人都赢了。"

他关于内容 vs 代码的最清醒观察：代码是工具，没人在意谁写的，只要它能运行。内容不同，因为内容承载着人的意图、品味和信任信号。"工作垃圾"不只是质量问题，更是身份认同问题。企业对 AI 生成内容的抵触不是非理性的——它是人们认为自己的工作"本该意味着什么"的反映。
