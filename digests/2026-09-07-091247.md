**No Priors: Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein**

**The Takeaway:** In the age of AI agents, your company's most valuable asset isn't its compute or its models — it's the decades of messy, locked-away operational data sitting in legacy systems, and the race is on to unlock it before competitors do.

EON co-founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein built their company on a counterintuitive insight: start with backup and disaster recovery — the most unglamorous corner of enterprise infrastructure — and you end up holding something more strategically valuable than any AI lab: a complete, structured copy of a company's entire data history. As models commoditize and compute costs fall, the only defensible moat is proprietary data.

The Google-Spirit Airlines story makes this concrete. Google paid $10 million for the bankrupt airline's data — not planes, not routes, not staff. Mercor reportedly also bid. The signal is unmistakable: companies that spent decades accumulating operational data didn't realize what they were sitting on. "It was usually like tapes, it was usually sitting on a shelf collecting dust. And all of a sudden this becomes very important."

But the more urgent concern is AI agent security. Ehrlich notes that six months ago, no enterprise executive was worried about "non-human actors" inside their own systems. Today, it's become the top-of-mind issue: AI agents with legitimate access credentials can drop database tables, expose sensitive data, or behave unpredictably — at machine speed. "We need to assume breach, whether it's malicious or not, and need to be able to handle it and act accordingly. It's a very weird situation today."

Their prescription: stop treating data infrastructure as a back-office IT problem, and start treating it as the CEO's most strategic asset — something that must be mapped, classified, secured, and continuously accessible before you can build anything meaningful on top of it.

**No Priors：重新思考传统数据基础设施——与 EON 联合创始人 Ofir Ehrlich 和 Gonen Stein 对话**

**核心观点：** 在 AI agent 时代，公司最有价值的资产既不是算力也不是模型，而是那些尘封在遗留系统中数十年的运营数据——谁能率先将它们解锁，谁就赢得了先机。

EON 联合创始人 Ofir Ehrlich 和 Gonen Stein 以一个反直觉的洞察创立了公司：从备份和灾难恢复——企业基础设施中最不起眼的角落——切入，最终握有的东西在战略价值上超过任何 AI 实验室：企业完整历史数据的结构化副本。随着模型日益商品化、算力成本持续下滑，唯一真正的护城河是专有数据。

谷歌收购 Spirit Airlines 数据的案例最能说明问题。谷歌花费 1000 万美元买下这家破产航空公司的数据，而不是飞机、航线或员工。Mercor 据报也参与了竞标。信号已足够清晰：那些花了几十年积累运营数据的公司，根本没有意识到自己坐拥宝藏。"这些数据过去就像磁带，搁在架子上落灰。突然之间，它们变得极为重要。"

但更紧迫的隐患是 AI agent 的安全问题。Ehrlich 指出，六个月前，没有任何企业高管担心系统内的"非人类行动者"。如今，这已跃升为首要关切：拥有合法访问凭据的 AI agent，可以以机器速度删表、泄露敏感数据或行为失控。"我们需要假设已发生入侵，不论是恶意的还是无意的，并做好应对准备。这是一个非常诡异的现实。"

他们的解法是：停止将数据基础设施视为后台 IT 问题，开始将其作为 CEO 最核心的战略资产来对待——在构建任何有意义的应用之前，必须先完成数据的映射、分类、安全管控，并保持持续可访问。
