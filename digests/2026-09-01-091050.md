**Training Data — Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed: Why AI Models Stop Learning, and How to Start It Again**

**The Takeaway:** Rich Sutton — who invented reinforcement learning, wrote its foundational textbook, and authored The Bitter Lesson — believes the field has made a foundational error: we built AI systems of remarkable capability that stop learning the moment they are deployed, and almost no one is working to fix it.

Sutton, co-founding Oak Lab with his former student Khurram Javed, argues that "continual learning" is a redundant phrase — all real learning is continual. The current LLM paradigm is, in his telling, a brilliant feat of language engineering that is nonetheless frozen in time: weights never change after training, and an agent that cannot update from experience is fundamentally limited regardless of how impressive its pre-training was. "I am not the weird one," he says. "The field is weird."

Their critique of synthetic data is equally sharp. The Big World Hypothesis, formalized by Javed, holds that the world is infinitely more complex than any agent or any simulation of it. Human engineers deciding what synthetic data to generate is itself the bottleneck — a drone learning to navigate via echolocation, for instance, requires training data that no human expert yet knows how to produce. "You can't even get synthetic data on anything," Sutton argues. "The world is infinitely complex."

The path forward they are pursuing at Oak Lab rests on two pillars. The first is continual deep learning — algorithms like continual backprop, published in Nature, that continuously inject small amounts of randomness (newly initialized units) into a network so it keeps learning without catastrophic forgetting. The second is self-discovered abstractions: agents that learn and plan with their own high-level representations of the world, rather than ones humans hand-designed. Their most ambitious target: a trillion-parameter mind running on 20 watts — achievable, they believe, within ten years as Moore's Law compounds.
https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOhHNjZItNnMm5tdW61JpnyxeYH5NDDx8

**核心洞见：** Rich Sutton——强化学习的发明者、该领域权威教材的作者、《苦涩的教训》一文的写作者——认为这个领域犯了一个根本性错误：我们建造了能力卓越的 AI 系统，但它们一旦部署就停止了学习，而几乎没有人在尝试解决这个问题。

Sutton 正与他的前学生 Khurram Javed 共同创办 Oak Lab。他认为"持续学习"这个词本身就是多余的——所有真正的学习都是持续发生的。在他看来，当前的 LLM 范式是语言工程上的杰出成就，但它从根本上是静止的：训练完成后权重就不再更新。无论预训练有多强大，一个无法从经验中更新自身的 agent 在本质上是受限的。"我不是那个奇怪的人，"他说，"这个领域才奇怪。"

他们对合成数据的批评同样直白。Javed 提出的"大世界假说"认为，世界的复杂程度远远超过任何 agent 或任何对它的模拟。人类工程师决定生成什么合成数据，本身就是瓶颈——例如，一架通过回声定位导航的无人机，如果还没有人类专家知道该为它生成什么样的训练数据，再多的合成数据也无济于事。Sutton 说："你根本无法为任何事物获得合成数据。世界是无限复杂的。"

他们在 Oak Lab 追求的解决方案围绕两个支柱。其一是持续深度学习——类似在 Nature 发表的"持续反向传播"（continual backprop）算法：持续向神经网络注入少量随机性（新初始化的神经元），使其能在不发生灾难性遗忘的情况下持续学习。其二是自主发现的抽象表示：agent 自主学习并利用对世界的高层次表示来规划行动，而不是依赖人类预先设计好的抽象。他们最雄心勃勃的目标：在摩尔定律的推动下，十年内用 20 瓦的功耗运行一个万亿参数的"心智"。
