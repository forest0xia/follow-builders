### Claude Blog: Claude in Chrome is generally available

**The Takeaway:** Claude can now autonomously take actions in your Chrome browser without asking for approval every step — and Anthropic has the safety evals to back it up.

Claude's browser extension is now available to all paid Claude plan users. The headline upgrade is autonomous action mode: Claude can now click, type, navigate, and fill forms without requiring your approval for each step, using the same safety mechanism as Claude Code's auto mode. A safety classifier reviews each action against your original request before it runs.

The more interesting story is how Anthropic got here. The original Claude in Chrome pilot (launched last year) was conservative by design — every action required explicit approval — because prompt injection remained a real risk. Hidden instructions in web content could redirect Claude against a user's intent. Since then, Anthropic has trained Claude against a growing library of attacks, deployed probes that scan web content before Claude acts on it, and added a safety classifier that blocks actions that don't match the user's request. On their current red-team evaluation using professional attackers: zero successful attacks against Claude Sonnet 5 or Opus 5 with probes and classifiers active.

Use cases Claude in Chrome now enables: working with internal dashboards with no connector, filling out forms on legacy systems, navigating vendor portals — anything requiring a browser where your existing logins are.
https://claude.com/blog/claude-in-chrome-generally-available

**要点：** Claude 现在可以在不逐步请求审批的情况下，在 Chrome 浏览器中自主执行操作——Anthropic 有安全评估数据作为支撑。

Claude 的浏览器扩展现已向所有付费 Claude 计划用户开放。最核心的升级是自主操作模式：Claude 现在可以不经过逐步确认，直接点击、输入、导航和填写表单，采用与 Claude Code 自动模式相同的安全机制。每个操作在执行前都会由安全分类器与用户最初的请求进行核对。

### No Priors: Redefining Chip Architecture with Arm CEO Rene Haas

**The Takeaway:** The CPU is not being replaced by the GPU — it's being redefined. ARM CEO Rene Haas argues that every AI workload, no matter how accelerated, still needs a CPU for orchestration, and that AI is now transforming how chips themselves are designed — compressing the 24-to-36 month design cycle from the inside.

**Why you should care:** ARM's IP is inside virtually every smartphone, most data center servers, and a growing share of AI accelerators. Haas has a front-row seat to the entire semiconductor supply chain — automotive, data center, hyperscalers, startups — making his view of what's actually constrained unusually grounded.

**Key insights from the episode:**

- **AI in chip design:** The biggest time sink in chip development isn't the architecture — it's verification, validation, and debug. AI excels at exactly that. ARM already has 80-90% of its engineers using AI tools daily. Haas's analogy: shutting off AI at ARM would be like going back to the 1990s and restricting internet access to two hours a day.

- **Why ARM built its own chip:** When Meta came to ARM wanting a general-purpose agentic CPU and no one else could deliver it, Meta and ARM built it together. The Arm GI CPU, unveiled at Hot Chips, was a direct result. Haas was careful to get sign-off from the ecosystem first — and Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, and Google all publicly congratulated the launch.

- **Supply chain bottleneck is long-term:** Haas doesn't think the current constraints on advanced packaging, wafers, memory, and substrates resolve in 12 or 24 months. He sees a constrained environment for three to five years, driven by the compute and memory intensity of transformer-based workloads.

- **Where AI tooling in chip design falls short:** RTL generation and physical implementation remain immature because the training data is largely proprietary. ARM's advantage: they have the richest documented IP portfolio in the industry — not just the IP, but the test benches and documentation that make it trainable.

- **Five-to-ten year outlook:** Haas thinks idea-to-GDS2-file (the file sent to the fab) automation is plausible for straightforward chip designs in five-plus years. Getting there for state-of-the-art designs ("design me something 10% faster than Verruvan") is a longer arc.

**Bottom line:** The CPU isn't commoditized — it's becoming more critical as the orchestration layer for heterogeneous AI systems. And the chip design process itself is being restructured by the same AI wave it enables.
https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast

**要点：** CPU 并未被 GPU 取代——而是被重新定义。ARM CEO Rene Haas 认为，无论计算如何加速，每一个 AI 工作负载都仍然需要 CPU 来进行调度协调；而 AI 本身正在从内部重塑芯片的设计方式——将原本长达 24 至 36 个月的设计周期从内部压缩。

**为什么值得关注：** ARM 的 IP 几乎存在于所有智能手机、大多数数据中心服务器以及越来越多的 AI 加速器中。Haas 对整个半导体供应链——汽车、数据中心、超大规模云厂商、初创公司——有第一手洞察，使他对当前真正瓶颈的判断格外接地气。

**本期核心观点：**

- **AI 在芯片设计中的应用：** 芯片开发中最耗时的环节不是架构设计，而是验证、确认和调试。AI 恰恰在这些方面表现卓越。ARM 目前已有 80-90% 的工程师每天使用 AI 工具。Haas 的类比：关掉 ARM 的 AI，就像回到 1990 年代把上网时间限制在每天两小时。

- **ARM 为何自研芯片：** 当 Meta 找到 ARM，想要一颗通用 agentic CPU，而市场上没有任何人能提供时，Meta 和 ARM 联手打造了它。在 Hot Chips 发布的 Arm GI CPU 正是这一合作的成果。Haas 事先征得了整个生态系统的认可——Nvidia、Amazon、微软和 Google 全都公开祝贺了这次发布。

- **供应链瓶颈是长期问题：** Haas 认为当前先进封装、晶圆、内存和基板的供需紧张，不会在 12 或 24 个月内缓解。他预计这种受限环境至少会持续三到五年，根源在于基于 Transformer 的工作负载对算力和内存的极高需求。

- **AI 工具在芯片设计中的短板：** RTL 生成和物理实现仍不成熟，原因是训练数据大多属于专有信息。ARM 的优势：拥有业内最丰富的文档化 IP 组合——不仅有 IP 本身，还有使其可训练的测试台和文档。

- **五到十年展望：** Haas 认为，对于结构相对简单的芯片，五年以上实现从概念到 GDS2 文件（发往晶圆厂的文件）的自动化是可能的。对于最先进的芯片设计（「帮我设计一颗比 Verruvan 快 10%、便宜 20%、能效高 30% 的芯片」），则需要更长时间。

**结论：** CPU 并未商品化——随着其成为异构 AI 系统的调度层，它正变得愈发关键。而芯片设计流程本身，也正在被它所赋能的那股 AI 浪潮所重塑。
