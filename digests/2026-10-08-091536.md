**AI & I by Every — "Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent"**

**The Takeaway:** After an early experiment with personal AI agents inside Every, the team realized the bigger opportunity wasn't one agent per person — it was one shared agent for the whole company.

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, has spent the past two weeks deep in OpenAI's Dots — persistent, proactive background agents that sit inside ChatGPT and act on your behalf. The use cases that emerged across Every's 30-person team are surprisingly mundane in the best way: filtering school emails, catching missed Slack access requests, and running errands while team members are on the move. What strikes Shipper most is that Dots protect attention. During weekend model testing, instead of getting sucked into Slack, he had his Dot surface only the responses that mattered. "I was able to post the results of all the experiments I was running in Slack. And if someone responded, my Dot would tell me about the response, which would help me stay up to date on what the team was thinking without getting me sucked into the whole rabbit hole of everything going on in Slack."

But the real story is what Every built for itself. After going through their own OpenClaw-based personal agent phase months earlier, the team realized the higher-leverage play was a company-wide agent — a single persistent layer that serves the whole organization rather than one person at a time. They built what they now call the Every Agent, and just released it to all their subscribers. Willie Williams, their head of platform, walks through what it was like to build at the frontier: the engineering tradeoffs, what personal agents taught them, and why teams that move to shared agents will operate differently from those still treating AI as an individual productivity tool.

Shipper's hot take on cute named agents: they don't survive contact with something better. His own is named Boo. "My experience using OpenClaw and other named agents is that as soon as something better comes along, you throw away the cute little character you develop." In a year, he expects persistent agents to be the default — just probably without the name.
