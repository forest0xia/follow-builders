**Claude Blog: Claude Code now supports artifacts**

Claude Code can now capture work progress as shareable, live visual pages — artifacts. These turn a session's output into web pages anyone can open and explore: PR walkthroughs, incident timelines, dashboards, release checklists, and more, with pages updating automatically as the session progresses and republishing to the same URL each time. Artifacts are built from the full session context including your codebase, connected tools, and conversation, and are private to your organization by default with role-based access controls. From the post: "With artifacts, team members and stakeholders don't have to 'walk us through what the agent found' because they're all looking at the same view, with the same context." Now in beta for Claude Team and Enterprise orgs, accessible via the Claude Code CLI and desktop app.
https://claude.com/blog/artifacts-in-claude-code

**Claude Blog：Claude Code 现已支持 artifacts**

**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 92: xAI Co-Founder Unpacks the Future of Model Development**

**The Takeaway:** Igor Babushkin, xAI co-founder and now founder of River AI, believes the AI industry is approaching a structural inflection point — closed-source model providers are getting squeezed from both sides, and the future belongs to distributed, personally-controlled AI rather than tokens served from centralized data centers.

Babushkin has had a front-row seat at every major AI breakthrough of the last decade: StarCraft and AlphaCode at DeepMind, early reasoning research at OpenAI, and the infrastructure build that became Colossus at xAI. At River AI, he's making three bets: a fine-tuning and RL API service (River API), deep personalization where models learn directly from each individual rather than averaged population preferences, and — most ambitiously — bringing frontier model inference to local hardware so users control their own AI without relying on any data center.

On the state of the industry, Babushkin is direct: "As a proprietary model builder you're kind of starting to get squeezed in a little bit. You make the model too good, you're not allowed to release it. But then open source is just right behind you, getting better and better every month." His prescription: the way out for OpenAI and Anthropic is to innovate again — explore new domains and new ways of generating revenue — because pure capability improvements are running into both technical diminishing returns and regulatory risk at the frontier.

His most urgent concern isn't AGI going rogue — it's AI amplifying inequality. "The most immediate urgent risk" is people feeling left behind while a few large companies control the most powerful models. His antidote, philosophically and commercially, is making AI personal and locally controlled.
