**No Priors — "What Chess.com Teaches Us About Superhuman Capabilities, with CEO Erik Allebest"**

**The Takeaway:** The most durable platform businesses are built not by outrunning incumbents but by obsessing over user experience for long enough that everyone else fades — and then doubling down when the market surprises you upward.

Erik Allebest bought the chess.com domain out of a bankruptcy auction in 2005 for $56,000 after every investor he met told him chess was "uninvestable." Two decades later, Chess.com has 250 million registered members, 10 million daily active users, and north of $200 million in annual revenue — all without a dollar of outside venture capital.

What's counterintuitive about how he built it: he never chased growth; he chased great UX. Free to play, browser-based, beautifully designed — and then he waited. The company grew at the speed of its own cash flow, hiring only when revenue allowed, avoiding the fundraising trap that forces premature scaling. "We always thought when we hit 50 people, we're never going to have more than 50 people on our team," he said. "Of course we blew past that. And we just stopped saying that."

On AI: Chess.com was actually the original benchmark for superhuman AI — computers have outclassed humans at chess for thirty years. Allebest is matter-of-fact about it: the game didn't die; it got more popular. He's now integrating AI deeply into the product — an "AI coach in your pocket" that tracks your game history and suggests personalized improvement paths — while staying clear that it's a complement to human coaching, not a replacement.

His most memorable line, on whether a chess rating can translate to poker: *"Losing a hundred dollars at the table, it's not my favorite thing, but I don't care that much. But losing 100 rating points on poker? That just really bothers me."*

Chess.com is now expanding into poker, applying its rating-system playbook to a game where financial stakes have always been the proxy for skill — and betting that players will care about their poker rating as much as their bankroll.

**No Priors——《Chess.com CEO Erik Allebest 谈超人类能力》**

**核心洞见：** 最持久的平台型产品，往往不是靠跑赢对手建立的，而是凭借对用户体验长达数年的执念，让竞争者一一凋零——当市场超预期爆发时，再乘势而上。

Erik Allebest 在 2005 年以 5.6 万美元从破产拍卖中买下了 chess.com 域名，彼时几乎每位投资人都告诉他，国际象棋是"无法投资的领域"。二十年后，Chess.com 拥有 2.5 亿注册会员、1000 万日活用户和超过 2 亿美元的年收入——全程没有接受过任何外部风险投资。

他的建构方式出人意料：他从未刻意追逐增长，只是死磕用户体验——免费、浏览器可用、设计精良——然后等待。公司以自身现金流速度扩张，有了收入才招人，彻底规避了过早扩张的融资陷阱。"我们总觉得到了 50 人就封顶了，"他说，"当然后来远远超过了。于是我们就不再说这种话了。"

在 AI 这个话题上：Chess.com 其实是人工智能超越人类的最早基准——电脑在国际象棋上击败人类已有三十年历史。Allebest 对此非常坦然：游戏并没有因此消亡，反而越来越流行。他现在正在把 AI 深度整合进产品——打造"放在口袋里的 AI 棋艺教练"，根据用户的历史对局给出个性化提升建议——同时强调，这是对人类教练的补充，而非替代。

最令人印象深刻的一句话，来自他对"国际象棋评级能否迁移到扑克"这个问题的回答：*"在牌桌上输掉一百美元，不是我最开心的事，但我不太在乎。但要是我的扑克评级掉了 100 分？那就真的很烦了。"*

Chess.com 正在扩展到扑克领域，将其成熟的评级体系引入这项长期以金钱作为技术代理指标的游戏——并押注于玩家最终会像重视筹码一样重视自己的扑克评分。
