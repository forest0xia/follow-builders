### AI & I by Every — "How a Professional Writer Writes With AI"

**The Takeaway:** The real unlock of AI for writing isn't speed — it's getting your brain out of the way of the work that only you can do.

Katie Parrott, a staff writer at Every, has spent two years evolving from AI skeptic to someone who builds her own writing plugins. Her journey began out of necessity: laid off from a crypto firm, unable to afford a career coach, she turned to ChatGPT at $20/month and found something she didn't expect — a tool for externalizing her thinking and breaking through paralysis.

What makes her approach distinctive is her insistence on the "last mile" problem in AI writing. She argues that AI's knowledge cutoff means it always works with stale data: "Our job as the humans is to close that last mile and provide the real world experiences that AI can't get." Paste a prompt without unique inputs — a study your company ran, a personal experience, a contrarian data point — and the model returns commoditized information. The ingredients are what make writing worth reading.

Parrott developed her context engineering approach before the term existed. She built brand style guides, audience personas, and competitor analyses — the same documents she produced as a content manager — and fed them to the model, reducing copy-paste manually before Claude Projects existed. Now those files live in structured projects. "The model is the kitchen," she explains. "The ingredients need to be fresh and good."

Her most personal insight: AI has also been a supportive technology for her mental health. Living with bipolar disorder and anxiety around email, she uses agents to handle the inbox sorting, book medical appointments she would otherwise postpone indefinitely, and even file insurance reimbursements. "AI kind of reduces friction in my life in a way that makes it easier for me to person."

The result of all this is the Compound Writing plugin she built (forking Kieran Klassen's Compound Engineering), now available on the Every GitHub. It structures writing into stages — brainstorm, outline, draft, substantive edit, line edit, final pass — and embeds literary frameworks from Vonnegut, Hitchcock, Sorkin, and Sedaris as reviewers to evaluate the work against their principles.

Her thesis for the upcoming Every Conference: AI's multiplicative value will compound disproportionately to the people who already have access, time, and economic comfort. "A much more compelling vision for an AI future is one where everyone is able to come along for the ride."

**核心观点：** AI 写作的真正突破不在于速度，而在于让你的大脑不再妨碍那些只有你才能完成的工作。

Every 专职作家 Katie Parrott 用了两年时间，从 AI 怀疑者变成了自己开发写作插件的人。她的转变始于一次无奈之举：从一家加密公司被裁员后，买不起职业教练，她以每月20美元的价格订阅了 ChatGPT，却意外发现了一种外化思维、打破心理僵局的工具。

她方法论的核心在于坚持解决 AI 写作中的"最后一公里"问题。她认为，AI 的知识截止日期意味着它永远在用过时的数据工作："我们作为人类的职责，就是弥合这最后一公里，提供 AI 无法获取的真实世界经验。"如果你只是甩出一个提示词，没有独特的输入——公司自己做的研究、亲身经历、反直觉的数据点——模型返回的只是被大量重复的泛化信息。是食材决定了写作是否值得一读。

Parrott 在"context engineering"这个词流行之前就开始实践这套方法。她建立品牌风格指南、受众画像、竞争分析——这些都是她做内容总监时的老本行——然后把它们喂给模型，在 Claude Projects 出现之前全靠手动复制粘贴。她形容说："模型是厨房，食材必须新鲜、优质。"

她最私人的洞察：AI 对她来说也是一种支持性技术，有助于应对心理健康问题。患有双相情感障碍和邮件焦虑，她用 agent 帮她整理收件箱、预约之前会无限期拖延的医疗门诊，甚至代她提交保险报销。"AI 减少了我生活中的摩擦，让我作为一个人能更顺畅地运转。"

这一切积累促成了她开发出 Compound Writing 插件（源于对 Kieran Klassen 的 Compound Engineering 的改写），现已在 Every GitHub 上开放。插件将写作结构化为：头脑风暴→大纲→草稿→实质性编辑→行文编辑→最终润色，并内置 Vonnegut、Hitchcock、Sorkin、Sedaris 等文学框架作为"评审人"，从不同维度评估写作质量。

她在即将举办的 Every 大会上的核心论点：AI 的乘数效应将不成比例地向那些本就拥有资源、时间和经济余裕的人倾斜。"一个更令人向往的 AI 未来，是让所有人都能搭上这趟车。"
