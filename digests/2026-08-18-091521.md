**Madhu Guru** (Senior Director of AI, Meta)

Meta's Madhu Guru offered a practical framework for building AI evals from scratch: start with a workflow you already know deeply, study actual user traces, define what good looks like at each step, and build test cases from real failure modes. Once you have a solid eval, make it repeatable and automatic—and keep it updated as user patterns evolve. He offered to turn this into a daily series.
https://x.com/realmadhuguru/status/2089480958571331623

Meta AI 高级总监 Madhu Guru 分享了从零构建 AI eval 的实操框架：从你最熟悉的工作流出发，研究真实用户 trace，逐步定义每个环节的"好结果"标准，并基于真实失败案例构建测试用例。有了扎实的 eval 后，让它可重复运行、自动化执行，并随用户行为模式的演变持续更新。他还表示愿意将此内容整理成系列文章每日更新。

**No Priors — "Chasing Trillion-Dollar Companies, Founder Ambition, Token Budgets, and Regulatory Capture with Sarah & Elad"**
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6l8oAO_LBx4

**The Takeaway:** The compressed five-year run that minted multiple trillion-dollar AI companies was a historical anomaly—and the best founders shouldn't let fear of AI labs push them toward timid, niche markets.

No Priors hosts Sarah Guo and Elad Gil unpacked the current venture climate with rare candor. The core argument: while the past five years produced a handful of trillion-dollar companies at unprecedented speed (Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX), reaching $50–100B in revenue as a single company is extremely difficult—and expecting multiple new ones in the next 3–5 years misreads history as a new normal. Elad's framework: it's less about market size ("is the TAM huge?") and more about whether a company can realistically achieve $50–100B in revenue within five years. Very few can.

Sarah pushed back with the "failure of imagination" problem: too many investors still use linear, per-seat TAM models when the evidence from coding—consumption-based, potentially 100x the current assumption—is already in front of them. Investors who intellectually accept "AI delivers services value" but act linearly are making a systematic error.

On founder ambition: Elad sees a troubling trend of the best founders retreating to niche markets to avoid competing with AI labs. His view: there are plenty of markets the labs won't dominate, and staying away from them out of fear is a mistake.

On exit timing: Elad proposed pre-scheduled, bi-annual board meetings dedicated to the question of whether to sell—non-emotional, non-pressured, just rational. His reasoning: "every year of AI time is like three to four years of normal cycle time," so the underlying facts change fast enough that founders need to check their thinking more often.

The episode opened with a striking regulatory analogy: France generates 70% of its power from nuclear with no major incidents; the US is at 18%, with no reactor built in forty years. A 1970s safety lobby effectively killed abundant clean energy. The parallel for AI regulation is left to the listener.

**综合要点：** 过去五年批量诞生万亿美元 AI 公司的速度是历史异常值，而最优秀的创始人不应因害怕 AI 大厂而退缩到过于细分的赛道。

No Priors 主播 Sarah Guo 与 Elad Gil 坦率地拆解了当前的风投格局。核心论点：过去五年以前所未有的速度催生了少数万亿美元公司（Anthropic、OpenAI、SpaceX），但作为单一公司达到 500–1000 亿美元收入极为困难——认为未来 3–5 年会再诞生多家万亿级公司，是把历史异常当成了新常态。Elad 的框架：问题不在于"市场空间够大吗"，而在于"有哪家公司能在五年内真正达到 500–1000 亿美元收入"。能做到的极少。

Sarah 则指出了"想象力失败"的问题：太多投资人仍在用线性的按席位 TAM 模型来看 AI 公司，但编程领域的案例——基于消费的商业模式、潜在的 100 倍增长空间——早已摆在眼前。那些口头接受"AI 交付服务价值"却依然线性行动的投资人，正在犯系统性的认知偏差。

在创始人野心方面，两人都十分直白：Elad 注意到一个令人担忧的趋势——最优秀的创始人正在退缩到细分赛道，以避免与 AI 大厂正面竞争。他的看法是：有很多大厂不会涉足的市场，因畏惧而主动回避是个错误。

关于退出时机：Elad 建议每半年设置一次预定的董事会议，非情绪化、非利益驱动地讨论"是否应该在接下来六个月内考虑出售"。理由是："AI 时间里的每一年，相当于正常节奏的三到四年"——底层事实变化之快，要求创始人更频繁地检视自己的判断。

本集以一个有力的监管类比开场：法国 70% 的电力来自核能，且无重大事故；美国仅有 18%，四十年没有建设新反应堆。七十年代的安全游说团体实际上扼杀了丰沛的清洁能源。这个类比对 AI 监管的影射，留给听众自行感受。
