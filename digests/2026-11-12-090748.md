### AI & I by Every — "A New Vision for Human Work After Automation"

**The Takeaway:** When everyone has access to the same AI, the irreplaceable human contribution isn't what you know — it's the perspective and responsibility you bring to what you look for.

Every CEO Dan Shipper closed his company's first-ever conference, Thesis: 2027, with a talk that reframes the scariest-looking AI capability chart into something more nuanced and empowering. Using OpenAI's GDPval benchmark — which shows AI beating human experts 83% of the time in 2026, up from 12.4% in 2024 — Shipper acknowledges the aggregate view looks alarming. But he argues the aggregate is misleading by design: it freezes humans at a 2020 baseline while measuring AI progress, which isn't how the future actually works.

His alternative is the "intimate" lens: zoom in on a real case, understand all the messiness, and extrapolate from there. His case study: last week, he analyzed a customer survey, built a live video game for his all-hands, spotted a team sales opportunity, vibe-coded a CRM, and started cold-emailing prospects — all in the margins of a conference week, using AI. In 2020 terms, that's the output of five people (data scientist, game developer, marketer, engineer, BDR) worth roughly $200K.

But here's what the aggregate misses. When his entire team got access to the same survey and the same AI, they each found something completely different and completely their own: Yash found a churn driver (too much email to new subscribers); Douglas found a psychographic insight (readers feel isolated in their AI enthusiasm); Jack made a Claymation game show. Claude's default response to the same data? Generic insights anyone could have gotten.

"Generation is really cheap, but ranking is hard," Shipper says. His head of evals found that Claude can predict what Shipper would say in Slack 93% of the time — but only if Claude gets 400 attempts. When asked to rank its own answers, Shipper's actual response lands in the top 10% only 60% of the time. Coin flip territory.

The implication is powerful: a team isn't just extra hands. It's multiple people, each responsible for different parts of the world, each using that responsibility to narrow their search space and their perspective to rank what matters. That's not replicable by default-prompting a model. And as AI capabilities spread and raise the floor for everyone, the differentiated value shifts entirely to your perspective and your responsibility.

His prescription: stop measuring AI only by how well it handles consensus problems (math, code, tasks everyone agrees are worth solving — all now commoditized). Start measuring how well it amplifies *your specific work, from your specific vantage point*.

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLuMcoKK9mKgHtW_o9h5sGO2vXrffKHwJL

**要点总结：** 当所有人都能用上同款 AI 时，真正无可替代的人类贡献不是你知道什么，而是你带着什么样的视角和责任感去寻找什么。

Every CEO Dan Shipper 在公司首届大会 Thesis: 2027 的压轴演讲中，将那张最让人恐慌的 AI 能力曲线图，重新诠释为一幅更加复杂和振奋人心的图景。OpenAI 的 GDPval 基准测试显示，2026 年 AI 在专家任务中胜过人类的概率已达 83%（2024 年仅为 12.4%）。Shipper 承认，宏观数据看起来确实令人震惊。但他认为，宏观数据在设计上就是有误导性的：它把人类冻结在 2020 年的基准线上来衡量 AI 的进步，而现实并非如此运作。

他的替代视角是"微观"视角：聚焦一个真实案例，理解所有的复杂性，再从中推演。他的案例是自己上周的经历：在一场大会的会议间隙，他分析了客户调研数据，为全员会议制作了一个实时视频游戏，发现了一个团队销售机会，用 vibe coding 搭建了一个 CRM，并开始给潜在客户发冷启动邮件。用 2020 年的标准衡量，这是五个人的工作量（数据科学家、游戏开发者、市场营销、工程师、BDR），价值约 20 万美元。

但宏观数据遗漏了关键的东西。当他整个团队拿到同一份调研数据和同一套 AI 工具时，每个人发现的东西都截然不同且独一无二：Yash 发现了用户流失的原因（新订户收到太多邮件）；Douglas 发现了一个用户心理特征（读者感到孤独，因为周围人觉得他们对 AI 的热情很奇怪）；Jack 制作了一个黏土风格的游戏节目来呈现调研结果。而 Claude 对同样数据的默认回答？是任何人都能得到的通用洞察。

"生成内容已经很便宜了，但排序很难。" Shipper 说。他的 evals 负责人发现，如果给 Claude 400 次机会，它 93% 的时间能预测出 Shipper 在 Slack 上会说什么。但当要求 Claude 对自己的答案进行排序时，Shipper 实际说的话只有 60% 的概率落在 Claude 认为的前 10% 里。基本是靠运气。

这个洞察的力量在于：团队不只是多几双手。而是多个人，每个人负责世界的不同部分，用这种责任感来缩小搜索范围，用各自的视角来判断什么重要。这是默认 prompt 一个模型无法复制的。随着 AI 能力持续普及并抬高所有人的基准线，差异化的价值将完全集中在你的视角和你的责任感上。

他的建议：不要只用 AI 解决共识性问题（数学、代码、大家都认同值得解决的任务——这些都已商品化）来衡量 AI。要开始衡量它能多好地从你的独特视角放大你的具体工作。
