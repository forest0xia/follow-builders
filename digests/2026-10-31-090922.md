### No Priors — "Taking on ServiceNow: Coding Agents the Rest of Us with Serval Co-Founder and CEO Jake Stauch"

**The Takeaway:** Enterprise AI adoption isn't blocked by AI capability — it's blocked by trust and permissions, and the companies that will win are the ones building governance rails, not the intelligence itself.

Jake Stauch co-founded Serval after years at Verkada watching IT teams complain about the same enemy: ServiceNow. Expensive, inflexible, perpetually six to twelve months behind. The insight that launched Serval: code is the right abstraction for enterprise automation, and the old drag-and-drop workflow builders are "too complex for nontechnical users and annoyingly abstracted for technical users." When AI made it possible for anyone to generate code, the blockers disappeared — except one. Enterprise access controls.

That remaining blocker became the product. Serval isn't trying to compete on intelligence. Its thesis is that as AI agents get more capable, enterprises will *tighten* permissions, not loosen them: "If you have access to all these agents, then the damage you can do with any kind of slightly elevated beyond normal permissions expands." Serval sits in the layer where permissions get escalated — every AI-triggered action goes through deterministic approval workflows, an audit trail, and a ticket. "Serval is the boundary. Serval is the guardrails."

What's counterintuitive: they don't baby IT teams. While most enterprise tools dumb things down, Serval gives IT full code visibility under the hood while abstracting away complexity for non-technical staff. Stauch: "IT is really interesting because they're closer to a software engineer than I think a lot of people give them credit for."

On background agents — the "ultimate moonshot" that everyone wants but nobody has cracked: "You want these to be very autonomous... On the other hand, autonomy is very scary in the enterprise." Their approach is incremental trust-building: the agent proposes specific deterministic workflows it wants permission to run; humans approve them; over time the agent becomes competent within a bounded toolkit without going unbounded.

Two and a half years in, Serval has grown from 2 to 200 people. Fox Corporation adopted it company-wide, and the internal champion who led the rollout was nominated MVP of the entire company — exactly the "career-making" narrative Stauch wants every Serval deployment to produce.

https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast

**要点：** 企业 AI 落地的瓶颈不在于 AI 能力本身，而在于信任与权限管理——最终胜出的，将是那些构建治理框架而非智能本身的公司。

Jake Stauch 在 Verkada 工作多年，见证了 IT 团队对 ServiceNow 的种种抱怨——昂贵、僵化、永远落后六到十二个月——由此萌生了创办 Serval 的想法。核心洞察是：代码才是企业自动化的正确抽象方式，而旧式的拖拽式工作流构建器"对非技术用户来说太复杂，对技术用户来说又抽象得让人恼火"。当 AI 使任何人都能生成代码，这些障碍就消失了——除了一个：企业访问控制。

这个剩余的瓶颈，成了 Serval 的产品。Serval 不在智能上与人竞争，而是押注于：随着 AI agent 能力增强，企业会收紧权限而非放宽——"如果你能访问所有这些 agent，那么任何略微超出正常授权的权限所能造成的破坏就会急剧扩大。"Serval 就坐在权限提升的这一层——每一个 AI 触发的操作都要经过确定性的审批流程、审计记录和工单。"Serval 就是边界，Serval 就是护栏。"

反直觉之处在于：Serval 不会把 IT 团队当成外行对待。大多数企业工具倾向于降低复杂度，Serval 却在底层保持代码完全可见，同时为非技术人员提供足够的抽象。Stauch 说："IT 其实比大多数人想象的更接近软件工程师。"

关于"终极 moonshot"背景 agent——人人都想要却无人真正解决：Serval 的思路是渐进式授权——agent 提议想要执行的具体确定性工作流，由人工审批，逐步在有限工具箱内积累能力，始终不越界。

成立两年半，Serval 已从 2 人成长为 200 人。Fox Corporation 全公司采用 Serval 后，主导该项目的内部负责人被提名为公司年度 MVP——这正是 Stauch 希望每次 Serval 部署都能带来的"助力晋升"叙事。
