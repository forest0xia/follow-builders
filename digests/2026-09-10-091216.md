**Aaron Levie — CEO, Box**

Box CEO Aaron Levie made two sweeping arguments today. On the scope of coding agents: they'll be used far more broadly than most expect — new software categories, affordable custom tooling for companies that previously couldn't afford it, cyber risk automation, life sciences research, and legacy system upgrades. His conclusion: lowering the cost of code drives demand for engineers *up*, not down. On the AI/GDP gap: the reconciliation is that AI diffusion will take much longer than assumed, constrained by corporate physics (data prep, process reengineering, change management) and real-world latency (regulatory timelines, clinical pipelines, customer response cycles). The opportunity, he argues, lies in building the bridges between superintelligence and real-world workflows.

**Dan Shipper — CEO, Every**

Every CEO Dan Shipper pushed back against a high-profile AI-and-jobs report, flagging two underexplored assumptions. First, the report assumes automation always reduces human labor — in his experience, automation frequently creates multiples more work for humans. Second, it treats jobs as decomposable task lists, missing what Shipper calls (drawing on Wittgenstein and Heidegger) the deeper reality: "a job is a particular way of seeing and caring about the world." As tools change, the same underlying cares generate entirely new tasks not captured by any prior list. Both gaps, he argues, should substantially alter how we reason about automation's economic impact.
https://x.com/danshipper/status/2097758891270697101

Every CEO Dan Shipper 对一份备受关注的 AI 与就业报告提出了质疑，指出其两个被低估的假设。第一，报告假设自动化总是减少人工——但他的亲身经历表明，自动化往往会成倍地增加人类的工作量。第二，报告将工作视为可分解的任务清单，却忽略了 Shipper 借用维特根斯坦和海德格尔所描述的更深层现实："一份工作是一种特定的看待世界与关心世界的方式。"随着工具的变化，同样的关切会催生全新的任务，而这些任务根本不在任何既有的清单上。他认为，这两个缺口足以从根本上改变我们对自动化经济影响的判断。
