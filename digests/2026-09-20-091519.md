**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — When AI Improves Itself | Richard Socher (Recursive)**

**The Takeaway:** Scientific progress has effectively stalled because human knowledge has fragmented into impenetrable silos — and Richard Socher believes AI-driven recursive self-improvement is the tool that will weave it all back together.

Richard Socher is one of the most cited AI researchers alive, an early pioneer of neural networks for NLP, and the founder of Recursive, which just raised $650M to build Recursive Self-Improving Superintelligence. He's also the author of *The Eureka Machine*, releasing September 22, which lays out a four-pillar framework for AI-accelerated scientific discovery.

Socher's core argument is that science hasn't slowed because of a lack of effort — it's slowed because knowledge has fragmented into thousands of specialized subfields, making cross-disciplinary insight nearly impossible. "We evolved from a body of knowledge to a labyrinth of knowledge, 34,000 journals that might as well have no trespassing signs." AI, he argues, is to biology what calculus was to physics — the language needed to weave complex systems back together.

His most counterintuitive take: hallucination in AI models isn't always a bug — it's sometimes a feature. When exploring novel protein sequences, a model that generates plausibly off-distribution outputs (higher temperature) is more likely to find genuinely new molecules than one that only memorizes known ones.

The $650M Recursive raised is mostly already committed: $410M went to a single AWS compute deal — and Socher treats that as likely their smallest compute purchase going forward. Their near-term focus is AI-for-AI research, building a system with the equivalent of 50,000 AI research PhDs, before eventually turning that capability toward biology, chemistry, and physics.

"Anything you can simulate, AI will solve. And before you know it, you're in this recursive self-improvement loop."

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — When AI Improves Itself | Richard Socher (Recursive)**

**核心观点：** 科学进步陷入停滞，是因为人类知识已经碎片化成一个个无法逾越的孤岛——Richard Socher 相信，由 AI 驱动的递归自我改进，正是将这一切重新编织在一起的工具。

Richard Socher 是当今被引用最多的 AI 研究者之一，NLP 神经网络的早期先驱，也是 Recursive 公司的创始人——该公司刚刚完成 6.5 亿美元融资，目标是构建递归自我改进的超级智能。他同时也是《The Eureka Machine》一书的作者，该书将于 9 月 22 日出版，提出了一套由 AI 加速科学发现的四支柱框架。

Socher 的核心论点是：科学发展放缓，不是因为缺乏努力，而是因为知识已经分裂成数千个专业子领域，让跨学科洞见几乎不再可能。"我们从一个知识体走向了一座迷宫，34000 本学术期刊，几乎每一本门口都挂着'闲人免进'的牌子。"他认为，AI 之于生物学，就如微积分之于物理学——是将复杂系统重新编织在一起所需要的语言。

他最反直觉的观点：AI 模型的"幻觉"并不总是缺陷，有时反而是特性。在探索新型蛋白质序列时，能够生成合理偏离分布（更高 temperature）的模型，比只会记忆已知结构的模型更有可能发现真正的新分子。

Recursive 融到的 6.5 亿美元大部分已有着落：4.1 亿美元用于与 AWS 的单笔算力协议——Socher 认为这很可能是他们未来最小的一笔算力采购。公司近期聚焦于"AI 研究 AI"，打造一套相当于拥有 5 万名 AI 研究博士能力的系统，再将这种能力应用于生物学、化学和物理学。

"任何可以模拟的事物，AI 都将解决。在你意识到之前，你已经身处这个递归自我改进的循环之中。"
