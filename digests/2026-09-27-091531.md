**No Priors — Why Diffusion Will Win AI Inference with Inception Co-Founder and CEO Stefano Ermon**

**The Takeaway:** Diffusion-based language models are poised to out-compete autoregressive LLMs at inference time — and Inception is betting that speed advantage will be the decisive factor in the next era of AI.

Stefano Ermon, a Stanford professor widely credited as one of the fathers of diffusion models, makes a deceptively simple case for his company Inception: the AI industry's obsession with next-token prediction will eventually hit the same wall that RNNs hit during training. The transformer won training efficiency because it parallelized computation across tokens. Diffusion models do the same for inference — generating many tokens simultaneously rather than one at a time — mapping far better to what GPUs actually do well. Autoregressive generation, by contrast, is fundamentally memory-bound and sequential, leaving most of the hardware sitting idle.

Inception's Mercury models now match the speed-optimized offerings from frontier labs in quality — on par with OpenAI's flash, mini, and nano models — while running significantly faster. One customer, Open Call (a voice AI company), switched from custom Cerebras chips to Mercury on standard NVIDIA GPUs and achieved comparable latency at lower cost and higher availability.

Ermon is precise about the market opportunity: he estimates 20–30% of current LLM workloads are latency-sensitive enough to benefit from diffusion models today, with that share growing as inference-time scaling becomes the dominant paradigm. "Once you get used to a fast model, it's hard to go back. It's kind of like broadband."

The deeper, less certain bet: diffusion models may also be more controllable than autoregressive ones. Because generation is coarse-to-fine rather than left-to-right, external reward functions can steer the output mid-generation — something structurally impossible for autoregressive models. Whether that advantage compounds at frontier scale is still an open question, and Ermon is candid about it. "We don't know what we're going to find. I doubt we've discovered the best way of building these systems."

**No Priors — 为什么 Diffusion 将赢得 AI 推理领域，与 Inception 联合创始人兼 CEO Stefano Ermon 对谈**

**核心洞察：** 基于 diffusion 的语言模型有望在推理阶段超越自回归 LLM，而 Inception 正在将速度优势作为下一个 AI 时代的决定性筹码。

Stefano Ermon，斯坦福大学教授，被广泛认为是 diffusion 模型的奠基人之一，为其公司 Inception 提出了一个看似简单却深刻的论点：AI 行业对 next-token prediction 的执念，终将遭遇 RNN 在训练阶段曾面临的同样瓶颈。Transformer 凭借跨 token 的并行计算赢得了训练效率；diffusion 模型则在推理阶段实现了同样的并行化——同时生成多个 token，而非逐个生成——从而更好地匹配 GPU 的实际计算特性。相比之下，自回归生成从根本上受内存带宽限制，且是顺序执行的，导致大量硬件算力处于闲置状态。

Inception 的 Mercury 模型如今在质量上已与前沿实验室的速度优化版本比肩——媲美 OpenAI 的 flash、mini 和 nano 模型——但运行速度更快。典型客户 Open Call（一家语音 AI 公司）从 Cerebras 定制芯片迁移到运行 Mercury 的标准 NVIDIA GPU，以更低成本、更高可用性实现了相当的延迟表现。

Ermon 对市场机会有着精准判断：他估计当前 20%–30% 的 LLM 工作负载对延迟已足够敏感，能够从 diffusion 模型中受益，随着推理时扩展成为主流范式，这一比例还将持续增长。"一旦习惯了快速模型，就很难回头，就像宽带一样。"

更深层、也更不确定的赌注：diffusion 模型可能也比自回归模型更易控制。由于生成过程是从粗到细，而非从左到右，外部奖励函数可以在生成过程中实时引导输出——这在结构上是自回归模型无法实现的。这种优势能否在前沿规模下复利增长仍是未解之题，Ermon 对此坦诚相告。"我们不知道会发现什么。我怀疑我们还没找到构建这些系统的最佳方式。"
