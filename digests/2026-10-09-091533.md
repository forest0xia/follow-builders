**Training Data — "Google's AI Infrastructure Chief, Amin Vahdat, on the Physics & Economics of Frontier AI"**

**The Takeaway:** Google measures its data center performance not by theoretical FLOPS, but by "goodput" — the actual useful work delivered per watt under real failure conditions — and it's this obsession with reliability at 100,000-accelerator scale that defines how Google thinks about AI infrastructure.

Amin Vahdat, Google's head of AI infrastructure, has spent years at the center of what he calls "the biggest CapEx build-out in human history." Google alone is expected to spend over $200 billion on infrastructure this year, and Vahdat's job is to ensure that investment actually delivers intelligence efficiently.

The most counterintuitive concept he introduces is "goodput." FLOPS are theoretical. Throughput counts all work done. Goodput only counts work that delivers the final answer. At 100,000-accelerator scale, something fails multiple times per hour — a chip, a network link, a compiler bug. Every failure means checkpointing, recovery, and restarting. Goodput per watt, not FLOPS, is how Google holds itself accountable: "What's the performance delivered by workload, under real failure conditions?"

On the TPU program, Vahdat explains why Google's 2013 bet on custom silicon — widely seen as contrarian — paid off. This year they released two chips: TPU 8i optimized for inference and TPU 8T for training. The logic was that inference is now 30-60% of total workload, making a specialized chip worthwhile. Both chips can still run the other's workload at lower efficiency, preserving flexibility.

The rise of long-horizon agents is changing data center design in a fundamental way: there's no longer a human in the loop to naturally rate-limit requests. What used to take seconds of human reading time now takes milliseconds between agent steps. This massively increases demand for CPUs, networking, and storage alongside accelerators — the traditional data center mix is coming back.

Power remains the single most fundamental constraint. Grid-connected is always preferred. A gigawatt build takes years of co-planning with utilities. As a moonshot, Google is seriously pursuing orbital data centers: sun-synchronous orbit offers 1.4x more solar energy with 90-100% sunlight coverage versus 28-35% on the ground — potentially transforming the power economics of AI compute.

"Using Gemini to design hardware for future Geminis as well." — Amin Vahdat

**Training Data — "Google AI基础设施负责人 Amin Vahdat 谈前沿 AI 的物理学与经济学"**

**核心洞见：** Google 衡量数据中心性能的标准不是理论上的 FLOPS，而是"goodput"——在真实故障条件下每瓦特实际交付的有效工作量。正是这种对 10 万块加速器规模下可靠性的执念，定义了 Google 对 AI 基础设施的思考方式。

Amin Vahdat 是 Google AI 基础设施负责人，多年来置身于他所称的"人类历史上规模最大的资本支出建设浪潮"之中。仅 Google 今年就预计投入超过 2000 亿美元用于基础设施建设，而 Vahdat 的职责就是确保这些投资能够高效地转化为智能输出。

他提出的最反直觉的概念是"goodput"。FLOPS 是理论值，throughput 统计所有完成的工作，而 goodput 只统计最终交付答案的有效工作。在 10 万块加速器的规模下，每小时都会发生多次故障——芯片、网络链路、编译器 bug 都可能出问题。每次故障都意味着检查点保存、恢复和重启。Google 以"每瓦 goodput"而非 FLOPS 作为自我问责的标准："在真实故障条件下，工作负载交付的实际性能是什么？"

关于 TPU 项目，Vahdat 解释了为何 Google 2013 年押注自研芯片——当时普遍被视为逆流之举——最终获得了成功。今年他们发布了两款芯片：针对推理优化的 TPU 8i 和针对训练优化的 TPU 8T。逻辑在于推理现在占总工作负载的 30-60%，使专用芯片变得值得。两款芯片仍可以较低效率运行对方的工作负载，保留了灵活性。

长时程 agent 的兴起正在从根本上改变数据中心的设计：循环中不再有人类来自然地限制请求速率。过去需要数秒人类阅读时间的间隔，现在 agent 步骤之间只需毫秒。这极大地增加了对 CPU、网络和存储的需求，传统数据中心的计算组合正在回归。

电力仍然是最根本的约束。Google 始终优先选择接入电网。一个吉瓦级的建设需要与公用事业公司共同规划数年时间。作为一项"登月计划"，Google 正认真探索轨道数据中心：日地同步轨道提供多 1.4 倍的太阳能，且拥有 90-100% 的日照覆盖率（地面仅 28-35%）——有望从根本上改变 AI 计算的能源经济学。

"我们正在用 Gemini 来设计未来一代 Gemini 所需的硬件。" — Amin Vahdat
