**Claude Blog: Claude Code now supports artifacts**

Claude Code can now capture session work as a living artifact — a shareable web page that updates in real time as your session progresses. Use cases include PR walkthroughs, incident investigation timelines, dashboards, system explainers, and release checklists that fill themselves out as work gets done.

The key design choice: artifacts are built from the full context of your session — your codebase, any connected tools, and the conversation itself. No data wiring required. Ask for a page and Claude Code builds it from what already exists. When the artifact is updated, the open link refreshes in place; teammates see changes the moment they're published, with version history and a gallery to manage everything.

Privacy defaults are organization-scoped: private to the author by default, shareable with org members, never publicly accessible. Admins get org-wide visibility, retention policies, and compliance API access.

This closes a real gap — teams that used to "walk someone through what the agent found" can now just share a link.

https://claude.com/blog/artifacts-in-claude-code

**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 92: xAI Co-Founder Unpacks the Future of Model Development**

**The Takeaway:** The person who helped build Grok and Colossus at xAI thinks closed-source AI companies are in a structurally difficult position — and left to start a company betting that personal, locally-run AI is the next frontier.

Igor Babushkin is one of those rare people who was present at every inflection point in modern AI: AlphaCode and StarCraft at DeepMind, early reasoning research at OpenAI, and then co-founding xAI where he did much of the infrastructure work behind the Colossus supercluster. In late 2025, he left to start River AI.

His diagnosis of where AI goes next is unusually specific. He believes the December 2025 coding agent moment — when Claude Opus convinced nearly everyone that agents were genuinely capable — was a turning point that will not stay contained to coding. But getting agents to work in non-verifiable domains (material science, novel physics, drug discovery) requires closing real-world feedback loops, not just more compute. "If you want to do great breakthroughs in the physical world, the agents need to be able to run experiments and get feedback as to whether that was a good idea."

On the business side, his take is pointed: "Power over AI is concentrating quite a bit. People aren't too happy about this. They don't necessarily trust the AI builders or feel like they have their best interests in mind." This concern is what shaped River AI's three bets: (1) a fine-tuning and RL service (the River API) that gives companies control over their models; (2) personalized AI that learns from individual users rather than training toward average behavior across populations; and (3) local inference hardware that would let a frontier-quality model run in your home without requiring data center access.

The third bet is the most ambitious — and the most candid about where the technology isn't yet: "Today you're very memory limited. You're not actually able to fit all of the weights of the model onto a single chip." But his argument is that latency and privacy unlock entirely new consumer experiences, particularly for voice and video-driven agents. He's running River like the early-era OpenAI and DeepMind: long time horizons, riskier bets, and a genuine expectation that new research will be required.

https://www.youtube.com/@RedpointAI

**The Takeaway（核心洞见）：** 曾参与构建 xAI Grok 和 Colossus 超算集群的联合创始人认为，闭源 AI 公司在商业结构上面临严峻挑战——他已经离开，并押注个人化、本地运行的 AI 是下一个前沿。

Igor Babushkin 是极少数亲历现代 AI 每个关键拐点的人：在 DeepMind 主导了 AlphaCode 和 StarCraft 的工作，在 OpenAI 参与了早期推理研究，随后联合创立了 xAI 并主导了 Colossus 超算集群的基础设施建设。2025 年底，他离开 xAI，创立了 River AI。

他对 AI 下一步走向的判断异常具体。他认为 2025 年 12 月的 coding agent 时刻——Claude Opus 让几乎所有人信服 agent 已真正具备能力——是一个不会局限于编程领域的转折点。但要让 agent 在不可验证的领域（材料科学、新物理发现、药物研发）真正发挥作用，需要与现实世界建立反馈闭环，而不只是更多算力。"如果你想在物理世界取得突破，agent 需要能够做实验，并获得关于这个想法是否有效的反馈。"
