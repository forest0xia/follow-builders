## PODCASTS

**No Priors — "The Changing Physics of Cybersecurity and Drug Development with OpenAI President Greg Brockman"**

**The Takeaway:** Greg Brockman believes we've crossed an irreversible threshold: AI can now generate genuinely new scientific knowledge — not just retrieve or recombine it — and security spending should ultimately vastly outstrip software spending.

OpenAI cofounder and president Greg Brockman joined No Priors hosts Elad Gil and Sarah Guo to discuss a week that included GPT-6 rollout to over a billion users and a landmark batch of math results. Brockman's framing is expansive: the recent resolution of nearly 400 long-standing mathematical problems represents not a parlor trick, but a qualitative shift in what AI can contribute to human knowledge. He draws a direct line from math to biology — the same techniques that work in checkable domains (where training signal is abundant) will progressively penetrate data-sparse fields like drug discovery, limited only by how quickly wet labs and simulation pipelines can generate feedback.

On cybersecurity, Brockman's argument is structural, not tactical: the attack surface in any large enterprise is already fragmented beyond human capacity to monitor. But the same properties that make this unmanageable for people — sprawling infrastructure, vast logs, enumerable attack vectors — make it tractable for agent swarms doing adversarial probing and fan-out testing. His claim is that security spending will need to massively outpace software spending as a result, though the precondition is modernized, unfragmented data infrastructure.

He also returned to a theme he's cared about since 2017: interpretability and debate techniques, which he argues are now practically relevant, not just theoretical. If AI produces proofs or insights no human can directly verify, the solution isn't blind trust — it's a second AI trying to falsify the first one, a technique that keeps humans meaningfully in the loop without requiring human-level expertise to adjudicate the result.

**No Priors —「网络安全与药物研发的物理规律正在改变：OpenAI 总裁 Greg Brockman 专访」**

**核心洞见：** Greg Brockman 认为我们已跨越了一个不可逆的门槛：AI 现在可以真正产生新的科学知识，而非仅仅检索或重组现有知识——与此同时，网络安全支出最终应该大幅超越软件支出。

OpenAI 联合创始人兼总裁 Greg Brockman 做客 No Priors，与主持人 Elad Gil 和 Sarah Guo 深入探讨了一个里程碑式的时刻——当周 GPT-6 面向超过十亿用户推出，同时 OpenAI 发布了一批重要的数学研究成果。Brockman 的视野宏阔：近期解决的近 400 道长期悬而未决的数学难题，绝非炫技，而是 AI 对人类知识贡献方式的质变。他将这一逻辑延伸至生物学——在数据充足的可验证领域行之有效的技术，将逐步渗透到药物研发等数据匮乏的领域，唯一的瓶颈在于湿实验室和仿真流程能多快产生反馈信号。

在网络安全议题上，Brockman 的判断是结构性的，而非战术层面：任何大型企业的攻击面，都已复杂到人力无法覆盖的程度。但正是这些让人类束手无策的特性——庞杂的基础设施、海量的日志、可穷举的攻击向量——反而让 agent 群组进行对抗性测试和大规模探查成为可能。他的预测是：随之而来的安全支出将大幅超越软件支出，但前提是企业必须先建立现代化、无碎片化的数据基础设施。

他还重申了一个自 2017 年以来始终萦绕的主题：可解释性与辩论技术（debate techniques）如今已不再是理论构想，而是切实可行的方案。如果 AI 产出的证明或洞见超出了人类直接验证的能力，解决之道不是盲目信任，而是让第二个 AI 去尝试证伪第一个——这一机制在无需人类具备专家水准判断力的前提下，仍然能保持人类对过程的实质性掌控。
