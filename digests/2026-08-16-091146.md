### AI & I by Every — "Why the Next Hit AI Product Will Be Social"
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dlI-5W7d7uU

**The Takeaway:** The next billion-user AI product won't be built by the most technically sophisticated team—it will be built by someone who deeply understands how people seek status, belong to communities, and learn from each other.

This episode features a conversation between Every CEO Dan Shipper and Sarah Tavel, a partner at Benchmark who was also an early employee at Pinterest. Tavel draws on her lens as an investor who has backed some of the biggest consumer social products of the last decade to ask a deceptively simple question: why haven't we seen a truly social AI product yet?

Her framework is compelling. She traces a historical arc from highly technical founding teams (Google, where the product *was* the engineering) to product-genius founders (Pinterest, Snap, Instagram, whose CEOs weren't deeply technical but understood people and social dynamics). Her thesis: AI is still in the "Google phase," where the model is the product and teams are necessarily deeply technical. But as infrastructure matures and costs drop—she cites DeepSeek as a potential inflection point—the window opens for product geniuses to build on top.

What's missing, in her view, isn't better models. It's the social layer. ChatGPT's Custom GPTs and Gems feel "criminal" to her—made by a brilliant team that doesn't have social DNA. The real opportunity is a UGC community where power users share their best prompts, workflows, and AI personas, letting others shortcut years of learning. She points to Reddit threads as an accidental proof of concept: people are already hunting for prompts there, but the experience is built for a different use case.

She also draws a distinction between "worky" AI (ChatGPT, Claude) and "personal" AI (Replika, Talinn), arguing there's a real product gap for something that occupies the personal bucket with genuine social mechanics—status, following, community.

For builders and investors watching AI's consumer layer: this episode is a useful map of where the product design frontier actually is, and what the next generation of AI companies might look like when the infrastructure is finally mature enough to let the product geniuses take over.

**The Takeaway（核心洞察）：** 下一个拥有十亿用户的 AI 产品，不会出自技术最顶尖的团队，而会来自那些深刻理解人类如何追求社会地位、归属感和相互学习的人。

这期播客是 Every CEO Dan Shipper 与 Benchmark 合伙人 Sarah Tavel 的对话。Tavel 曾是 Pinterest 的早期员工，也是过去十年多个顶级消费社交产品的投资人。她提出了一个看似简单却极具穿透力的问题：为什么我们至今还没有看到一个真正意义上的"社交 AI 产品"？

她的分析框架颇具说服力。她描绘了从"极度技术型创始人"（如 Google，产品即工程本身）到"产品天才型创始人"（如 Pinterest、Snap、Instagram，CEO 不一定技术深厚，但极懂人性与社群）的历史演变弧线。她的核心论点是：AI 目前还处于"Google 阶段"——模型即产品，团队必然深度技术化。但随着基础设施成熟、成本下降（她认为 DeepSeek 可能是一个拐点），产品天才们将迎来真正的机会窗口。

在她看来，缺失的不是更好的模型，而是社交层。ChatGPT 的 Custom GPTs 和 Gems 让她感到"有些浪费"——背后是极其优秀的团队，但缺乏社交 DNA。真正的机会在于构建一个 UGC 社区，让高阶用户分享他们最好的 prompt、工作流和 AI 角色，帮助其他人跨越漫长的学习曲线。她指出 Reddit 上的 prompt 搜索行为，是一个意外的市场验证——人们已经在那里找 prompt 了，只是那个平台并非为此而生。

她还区分了"工作型 AI"（ChatGPT、Claude）和"个人型 AI"（Replika、Talinn），认为市场上存在真正的产品空白：一个占据"个人"象限、同时具备真实社交机制——地位、关注、社群——的 AI 产品。

对于正在关注 AI 消费者层的创业者和投资人：这期播客是一张有用的地图，指向产品设计前沿的真实位置，以及当基础设施终于足够成熟，产品天才们接手时，下一代 AI 公司可能的样貌。
