### Anthropic Engineering

**How we contain Claude across products**
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude

Anthropic's engineering team published a frank deep-dive into the security architecture behind three of their agentic products: claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork. The core tension: as agent capabilities expand, so does blast radius — and the question shifts from "should we deploy?" to "how do we cap the damage if something goes wrong?"

The post documents three isolation patterns matched to user sophistication. claude.ai uses ephemeral gVisor containers with server-side execution — minimal blast radius, minimal capability. Claude Code uses an OS-level sandbox (Seatbelt on macOS, bubblewrap on Linux) that reduced permission prompts by 84% while granting developers a largely uninterrupted workspace. Claude Cowork runs a full VM, because non-technical knowledge workers shouldn't be expected to evaluate bash commands.

Three incidents taught them the most: (1) Pre-trust-dialog hook execution — Claude Code was reading project configs before asking "do you trust this folder?", enabling malicious repository hooks to fire automatically; (2) A controlled red-team phish that exfiltrated AWS credentials 24 out of 25 tries via a paste-and-run prompt — "the only defense that holds in this situation is the environment"; (3) Exfiltration through an approved domain, where a malicious file instructed Claude to use an attacker's API key to upload files to api.anthropic.com — which the egress allowlist correctly passed.

The recurring lesson: "the weakest layer is the one you built yourself." Battle-tested hypervisors and container runtimes held. Custom proxies and allowlists failed.
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude

**An update on recent Claude Code quality reports**
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem

Anthropic published a detailed postmortem on three separate changes that degraded Claude Code quality over the past month, all now fixed as of April 20 (v2.1.116). The three issues: (1) Claude Code's default reasoning effort was silently downgraded from `high` to `medium` on March 4 to reduce tail latency — a tradeoff users rejected loudly, reverted April 7; (2) A caching optimization introduced March 26 contained a bug where thinking history was cleared every turn (not just once after an idle hour), making Claude seem forgetful and repetitive, and burning through usage limits faster; (3) A system prompt change shipped April 16 to reduce verbosity — "keep text between tool calls to ≤25 words" — caused a 3% intelligence drop across eval sets and was reverted April 20.

Because each change hit a different slice of traffic on a different schedule, the combined effect looked like "broad, inconsistent degradation." As a result, Anthropic is resetting usage limits for all subscribers today, adding per-model eval suites for every system prompt change, and moving toward broader internal staff usage of the exact public build.
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem

**Scaling Managed Agents: Decoupling the brain from the hands**
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

Anthropic Engineering shares the architecture behind Managed Agents, a hosted service for running long-horizon agents at scale. The key design insight: the original single-container architecture — where the session, harness, and sandbox all lived together — created a "pet" that had to be nursed back to health when it failed. The fix was to decouple the "brain" (Claude + harness) from the "hands" (sandboxes and tools) and the "session" (the durable event log).

With the brain and hands separated, containers became cattle: if one fails, the harness catches the error as a tool-call result and Claude can retry with a fresh container. The harness itself became cattle too — since the session log lives outside it, a crashed harness can be rebooted via `wake(sessionId)` and resume from the last event. This architectural separation also eliminated a security failure mode: credentials never enter the sandbox where Claude's generated code runs.

The performance gains were significant: p50 time-to-first-token dropped roughly 60% and p95 dropped over 90%, because containers are now provisioned on demand rather than up front for every session.
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

**Anthropic Engineering：扩展 Managed Agents——将"大脑"与"双手"解耦**
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

Anthropic 工程团队分享了 Managed Agents 服务的架构设计，这是一项用于大规模运行长周期 agent 任务的托管服务。核心设计洞察是：最初的单容器架构将 session、harness 和 sandbox 全部放在一起，等于养了一只"宠物"——容器一旦出故障，就得人工维护。解决方案是将"大脑"（Claude + harness）与"双手"（sandbox 和工具）以及"记忆"（持久化事件日志）完全解耦。

大脑与双手分离后，容器变成了"牲口"：某个容器失败时，harness 将其作为工具调用错误捕获，Claude 可以用新容器重试。harness 本身也变成了"牲口"——由于 session 日志独立存储，crashed 的 harness 可以通过 `wake(sessionId)` 重启并从上一个事件断点恢复。这种架构分离同时解决了一个安全隐患：凭证再也不会进入 Claude 生成代码运行的 sandbox。

性能提升显著：p50 首 token 延迟下降约 60%，p95 下降超过 90%——因为容器现在按需供应，不再为每个 session 预先启动。
https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/managed-agents

### AI & I by Every — "How a Professional Writer Writes With AI"

**The Takeaway:** AI didn't just make Katie Parrott faster — it fundamentally changed the ceiling of what she can produce, while also becoming the tool that helps her manage bipolar disorder and function day-to-day.

Katie Parrott, staff writer at Every, started using AI as a career coach when she was laid off from a crypto firm with no budget for a real coach. That decision led her to Every, and two years later, she's both writing prolifically with AI and building her own tools on top of it.

Her core insight on writing with AI: the model isn't the variable — the inputs are. "When people are writing with AI, they just kind of go with two and say, 'write a blog post about style guides'...And they don't give it anything to go on. But then what the model already knows is commoditized information." The fix is to close "the last mile" by feeding AI the fresh data it doesn't have: original research, real customer interviews, personal experience, current performance metrics.

She built the Compound Writing plugin by forking Kieran Klassen's Compound Engineering plugin and adapting it for writing workflows — brainstorm, outline, draft, substantive edit, line edit, final pass — with review modes borrowed from Vonnegut (eight story elements), Hitchcock (suspense mechanics), Sorkin, and Sedaris. Her characterization of a great plugin: "an expert instruction manual for how to do something you have no business doing."

What's less expected: Katie also uses AI as a mental health tool. With bipolar disorder, she finds AI reduces friction on daily tasks that would otherwise derail her — inbox management, scheduling appointments, project prioritization through voice monologue. "AI hasn't just helped me produce content faster, it has fundamentally changed the scale of what I can do. The limits of what I used to bump up against — time, energy, capacity — are way lower."

Her thesis heading into the Every Conference: the compounding benefits of AI will concentrate with people who already have access, time, and economic comfort to experiment. "A much more compelling vision for an AI future is one where everyone is able to come along for the ride."

### AI & I by Every — "How a Professional Writer Writes With AI"

她基于 Kieran Klassen 的 Compound Engineering 插件，开发了自己的 Compound Writing 插件，将写作流程拆分为脑暴、大纲、起草、实质性修改、逐行润色、最终定稿，并内置了借鉴 Vonnegut（八大故事要素）、Hitchcock（悬念机制）、Sorkin、Sedaris 的审稿视角。她对优秀插件的定义是："一本专家级操作手册，让你能做原本根本没资格做的事。"
